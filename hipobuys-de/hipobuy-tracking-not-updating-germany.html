Hipobuy Tracking Not Updating in Germany? Read the Last Scan Before You Escalate

A quiet tracking page does not tell you what happened. The useful question is where the parcel was last physically scanned, who controlled it at that moment, and what evidence you can preserve before asking for help.

Published 9 August 2026Fact-checked 9 August 2026Independent guide

The five-minute triage

  1. Copy the parcel number rather than the product-order number.
  2. Record the exact last scan, location and timestamp.
  3. Decide whether it is a label, export, customs, handoff or delivery-stage gap.
  4. Check the carrier named for that stage, not every tracker on the internet.
  5. Save a compact evidence pack before contacting the party that can act.

Start with the tracking event, not the number of silent days

When a Hipobuy parcel appears stuck, counting days is tempting but incomplete. Two parcels can show no update for the same period while needing completely different actions. One may only have an electronic label. Another may have left an export facility and be travelling between networks. A third may already be in Germany but waiting for an address correction or import decision.

Read the last event literally. Separate data creation from physical possession. A message such as “shipment information received” usually proves that data was transmitted; it does not, by itself, prove that a carrier accepted the box. A processing or departure scan is stronger because it ties the parcel to a place and time. A customs or delivery-exception scan is stronger still because it identifies the current problem category.

This distinction prevents the most common escalation mistake: asking a German last-mile carrier to investigate a parcel it has not yet received. It also prevents the opposite mistake—waiting on an agent dashboard when the local carrier is already asking the recipient for an action.

Classify the last scan into one of five stages

International tracking is often a chain of systems rather than one continuous diary. Germany’s DHL guidance explains that an international partner may not always pass every destination event back to the origin system. That is why the same number can look quiet in one interface while the destination carrier shows a newer scan.

Check whether the number belongs to the parcel you are diagnosing

Shopping-agent orders create several identifiers. A product order can cover the seller-to-warehouse movement. A consolidated parcel has its own submission or parcel reference. The logistics provider may then create an international tracking number, and a destination partner may expose another number. Copying the wrong identifier can make a healthy shipment look invisible.

  • Match the scope: confirm that the number belongs to the outbound parcel, not one item inside it.
  • Match the format: preserve letters, leading zeros and suffixes when copying.
  • Match the carrier: use the carrier identified in the parcel record or tracking event rather than relying on automatic detection.
  • Match the destination: check that Germany and the correct postal code appear in the parcel summary, without sharing the address publicly.

DHL’s German help pages note that a tracking number is not an order number and that a newly created shipment can take time to appear. Treat that as a carrier-specific explanation, not a universal HipoBuy deadline. Other lines and postal partners can expose events differently.

Understand the three gaps that look the same on screen

1. The first-scan gap

The parcel record says dispatched or provides a number, but the carrier only shows electronic information. The missing fact is physical acceptance. Save the dispatch timestamp and the carrier status together. If the gap persists beyond the route’s current instructions, the useful question for the sender is: “What is the first physical acceptance scan?”

2. The line-haul gap

The last scan is a real departure or export-processing event, followed by silence. Parcels can travel between facilities without a customer-visible scan. DHL explicitly says some processing statuses can remain unchanged for hours or days even while a shipment continues moving. Do not translate silence into loss. Look for the next expected type of event—arrival, import processing or partner handoff—without promising when it will appear.

3. The destination-data gap

The parcel may have reached a German partner, but the origin tracker has not received the partner event. Search the official destination carrier using the number supplied in the parcel record or a cross-reference shown by the carrier. Avoid posting a full tracking number on social media or public review pages.

Customs silence and customs action are not the same

A status mentioning import or customs does not automatically mean seizure, tax due or missing paperwork. The important distinction is whether the tracker shows routine processing or a clear request. Check the parcel account, email and official carrier page for an action message. Preserve the product description, declared parcel information and any document request, but do not send identity documents through an unverified channel.

If the status says that delivery cannot continue because of incomplete information, act on the stated problem. German DHL guidance lists missing or insufficient customs documents, unpaid import charges, restricted contents and missing electronic data among possible reasons an international parcel cannot be delivered. Those are possible categories, not a diagnosis of your parcel.

Decision rule: routine import processing calls for monitoring; an explicit request calls for a documented response; a return-to-sender event calls for immediate preservation of the reason and contact with the sender-side account.

Build an evidence pack that a support team can actually use

A long emotional message usually creates more work than a short timeline. Before contacting support, assemble one private record. It should contain the parcel reference, tracking number, route name as displayed, destination country, dispatch date, exact last event, last event time zone, last event location, and screenshots of the parcel and carrier pages. Add the order numbers only when support needs to identify the contents.

Then write one clear request:

  • “Please confirm whether the parcel received a physical carrier acceptance scan.”
  • “Please identify the current carrier and any destination tracking number.”
  • “Please confirm whether the latest status requires action from the sender or recipient.”
  • “If an investigation is appropriate under the purchased route, please state who must open it and what evidence is required.”

Do not claim insurance coverage, a refund deadline or a guaranteed reshipment unless the terms attached to that exact parcel support it. Route names, protection options and investigation rules can change. Save the terms visible at purchase and parcel submission rather than relying on a screenshot from another buyer.

Escalate to the party that controls the current stage

If there is no physical acceptance scan, the sender-side parcel account is normally better placed to verify handover. If the parcel has a real international departure but no destination event, the sender may need to ask the contracted logistics provider. If a German carrier has scanned the parcel and shows an address or pickup exception, the recipient can often follow that carrier’s official instructions directly. If customs requests documents from the declarant or sender, forwarding the request to the appropriate party is more useful than opening unrelated tickets.

For a DHL local leg, current German guidance says a shipment can be investigated when its status has not changed for more than five working days, and it recommends that a recipient expecting an online order first contact the sender. That is a DHL rule for the relevant DHL stage, not a universal waiting period for every HipoBuy route. Use the current rules of the carrier that actually has possession.

A worked example: quiet export scan versus local exception

Imagine Parcel A last shows “departed export facility.” No German delivery partner is listed and no action request appears. The evidence proves origin-network movement, not German-carrier possession. The buyer records the event, checks for a later destination number and asks the sender-side account to confirm the contracted carrier if the route’s support window is reached.

Parcel B last shows a German delivery-facility scan followed by “address information required.” Waiting for another international scan would miss the problem. The buyer compares the postal code and house number with the parcel summary, follows the official carrier’s correction process where available, and keeps confirmation of the update.

Both screens can remain unchanged for several days. The correct action comes from the type of last scan, not from copying a generic “wait seven days” rule.

Final checklist before you send a ticket

  • You are using the parcel tracking number, not a product-order reference.
  • You copied the exact last event, timestamp, location and carrier name.
  • You classified the gap as label, export, handoff, customs or local delivery.
  • You checked the official tracker for the carrier controlling that stage.
  • You verified the destination postal code privately.
  • You saved the route terms and parcel-submission record.
  • Your message asks one concrete question and contains no public personal data.
  • You have not assumed loss, customs seizure, insurance eligibility or a refund outcome.
