HipoBuy Germany Customs and Tax Planning: A 2026 Evidence Checklist

A parcel value is not a number to improvise at dispatch. German import treatment depends on what the goods are, what the consignment contains, how the value is supported and which rules are current when it enters the EU. Build the evidence before the box leaves the warehouse.

Published and fact-checked 27 August 2026 · Independent buyer guide

A parcel value is not a number to improvise at dispatch. German import treatment depends on what the goods are, what the consignment contains, how the value is supported and which rules are current when it enters the EU. Build the evidence before the box leaves the warehouse.

Start with the 2026 rule change, not an old forum threshold

Germany’s customs administration now states that the previous customs-duty exemption for commercial consignments worth up to €150 ended on 1 July 2026. Its current internet-order guidance says a €3 customs duty applies per item in consignments with a total value up to €150, while other goods can follow the normal tariff rules. Import VAT and any carrier handling charge are separate questions. A spreadsheet or an old parcel screenshot that predates July 2026 is therefore not a safe customs calculator.

Treat this as a planning trigger rather than a universal final bill. The classification of the goods, number of items, value basis, origin, applicable reliefs and the way the shipment is declared can affect the outcome. HipoBuy’s public app description confirms a China-purchase and international-shipping workflow, but it does not replace German customs guidance or give an item-specific tax ruling. Confirm the live parcel declaration screen, then use the current German customs information for the destination.

Evidence rule: every amount in your plan needs a label: confirmed product value, estimated import charge, current platform quote, or amount actually collected. Do not combine those categories into one unexplained “tax included” figure.

Build a line-by-line customs ledger before consolidation

Begin with one row for each ordered item. Record a plain product description, quantity, purchase amount, domestic delivery allocated to that item, the source currency, payment date and the evidence that supports the amount. “Clothes” is too vague for your own audit. “One cotton-blend hooded sweatshirt, adult size M” is useful because it identifies the type without marketing language. Do not copy a seller title filled with model codes if it hides what is actually inside.

Preserve the order confirmation and payment record privately. If a product had a discount, keep the pre-discount amount, the discount and the amount paid rather than saving only a cropped total. If several items share one payment, retain the item-level breakdown. Currency conversion should also be dated: the amount charged by a payment provider is evidence of what you paid, while a later search-engine exchange rate is not.

Separate product value, logistics and border charges

A useful cost model has distinct layers. The warehouse purchase layer contains the item price and any seller-to-warehouse delivery. The parcel layer contains packing services and international shipping. The border layer contains import VAT, customs duty when applicable and a possible operator disbursement or presentation fee. Keeping layers separate prevents a buyer from blaming customs for a carrier fee or treating international freight as if it were the product price.

Do not publish one percentage as the answer for every parcel. Germany commonly applies import VAT to imports, but the taxable basis and applicable rate depend on the goods and the transaction. German customs guidance is the right place to confirm the current treatment; a calculator on an unrelated shopping site is not. For goods with special rates, excise treatment, intellectual-property concerns, food, medicines or animal and plant materials, obtain category-specific guidance before ordering.

Do not relabel a purchase as a gift. German Customs distinguishes genuine private gifts from commercial internet orders. A product bought from a seller is not transformed into a private gift because the recipient hopes to use a gift threshold.

Model three scenarios instead of one false-precision total

Create a base scenario using the current purchase values and the shipping quote visible for the planned parcel. Then create a higher-cost scenario that allows for a different classification outcome, currency movement, a carrier collection fee or revised parcel measurement. Finally, create a stop scenario: the amount at which you would not submit the parcel and would instead reconsider the contents or request professional advice. The stop line matters because a budget without a decision rule only records anxiety.

Keep the €3-per-item low-value duty in its correct context and date it. Do not multiply it blindly across every product row if the consignment falls under another tariff rule or an exclusion. The purpose of the model is to expose which questions need confirmation. If your result changes materially depending on whether a set is counted as one item or several, that is a classification question to resolve rather than a number to choose.

  • Use the current rule date and destination, not the date an article was bookmarked.
  • Keep each item and quantity visible in the ledger.
  • Mark shipping, VAT, duty and handling as separate lines.
  • Record every unknown rather than filling it with zero.
  • Set a stop amount before parcel submission.

Check descriptions against the physical warehouse evidence

The declaration record should describe the received items, not an abandoned option or a spreadsheet thumbnail. Compare the selected variant with the warehouse intake and visible photographs. Confirm quantity, product type and any component that changes classification. A bag with a removable battery, a cosmetic containing liquid or a garment made with restricted animal material is not adequately represented by a generic category word.

This is also where customs planning and route eligibility meet. An item can be legal to own yet restricted by a carrier, or it can require documentation the chosen route cannot accept. Do not assume a route shown for one ordinary garment applies to every item in a mixed parcel. If a product description, material or component remains uncertain, hold it out of the parcel until the evidence is sufficient.

Preserve a privacy-safe handoff file

Your full customs file may contain name, address, payment references and order identifiers. Keep it private. For your own checklist, use short internal item codes and redact personal information before asking a public question. Support should receive the minimum information necessary through the current authenticated channel, not through a public post. Store the final parcel summary, declaration, quote and any carrier payment notice together.

If customs or the carrier requests clarification, answer the precise question with consistent documents. Do not send several different value tables or descriptions. Note when the request arrived, which item it concerned, what you supplied and the response. A clean chronology is more useful than a long message about what you expected to happen.

Final pre-submission decision

Before approving the parcel, ask five questions. Does every item have a neutral description and supported value? Does the item count match the warehouse record? Were the German rules checked after the July 2026 change? Are VAT, duty and carrier fees represented as separate possibilities? Is the selected route eligible for every item? If any answer is unknown, pause at that question.
