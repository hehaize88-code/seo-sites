Hoobuy Damage and Missing Parts Evidence

Stains, damage and missing parts require different proof. A stain is a surface condition, damage changes the item’s structure or finish, and a missing part is an inventory mismatch. Combining them into one vague complaint weakens the record. The strongest warehouse review identifies the exact category, locates the issue and ties it to the ordered contents before any parcel is submitted.

Quick inspection plan

  • Start with a whole-item image that establishes orientation.
  • Add a close-up and a second angle for ambiguous marks.
  • Use the listing and order record to define what parts should be included.
  • Describe location, scale and effect without guessing the cause.
  • Preserve original images and support timestamps.

Classify the issue

Use “stain” for discoloration or residue, “damage” for tears, cracks, dents, broken hardware or permanent deformation, and “missing” only when an expected component is absent from the complete inventory view. Scratches, glue traces, loose threads and packaging dents should be described separately because their consequence differs.

Classification helps choose the next image. Surface marks need lighting and scale; structural damage needs side views and load-point context; missing parts need a full layout plus the listing’s included-items record.

Document stains and marks

Mark the location using stable landmarks such as a seam, pocket, logo or edge. Request an evenly lit close-up with enough surrounding area to orient the viewer. A second angle helps distinguish residue, shadow, pile direction and a true colour change.

Do not assume a mark will wash out or is permanent. Images rarely establish that. State its approximate size only when a reliable reference is in the same plane, and avoid pixel-based measurements from perspective photos.

Document structural damage

For cracks, tears, dents or bent parts, capture the whole component and both sides of the affected area when possible. Check nearby fasteners, seams and edges because the visible mark may extend into a load point. If the product includes moving parts, confirm whether a specific functional check is currently available.

Describe the visible consequence: gap, split, displaced hinge, exposed filling or loss of alignment. Avoid assigning a cause such as factory damage or warehouse handling unless the record actually supports it.

Prove a missing component

Create an expected-parts list from the paid order and seller listing. Then compare it to one overview showing all received contents. Small parts may be inside a case, pocket or box, so ask for an interior check before concluding they are absent.

Optional promotional items and listing props are not always included. Base the claim on explicit included-content language or the selected variant, not only on a product photograph.

Submit a concise resolution request

Lead with the order ID and issue class, attach the order evidence, overview and detail, then state the preferred resolution. Keep one issue per numbered line. This lets support confirm which image supports which claim.

Check the current workflow and deadline in the live account. If the evidence is still incomplete, request the smallest missing view promptly. A beautiful dossier submitted after the applicable window is less useful than a clear, timely record.

Evidence strength ladder

Read every warehouse finding on a four-level ladder. Confirmed means the relevant label, component or defect is clearly visible in context. Consistent means several views support the same interpretation but a small limitation remains. Unclear means focus, angle, lighting or coverage prevents a reliable conclusion. Not shown means the required area is absent. Only the first two levels should normally support a confident item description; the other two describe an evidence gap, not a failed product.

Keep severity separate from certainty. A clearly visible minor packaging crease may be confirmed but low impact. A hidden connector may be unconfirmed yet high impact because compatibility depends on it. This two-axis approach prevents dramatic language from replacing evidence and helps direct the next action.

  • Method: Use this control during review — Use the listing and order record to define what parts should be included.
  • Timing: Use this control during review — Describe location, scale and effect without guessing the cause.
  • Record: Use this control during review — Preserve original images and support timestamps.

Another common mistake is treating silence as proof. If a view does not show a feature, mark it “not shown”; do not convert the absence of evidence into a pass or failure. Also avoid comparing a warehouse image with a heavily edited seller image as though both were calibrated records. Use construction landmarks, labels and observable condition wherever possible.

A five-minute final audit

Read the order option aloud, then point to the image that confirms it. Scan the item from whole shape to labels, surfaces, edges, hardware and included parts. Circle any conclusion that depends on an assumption. For each circle, decide whether a new image, a live-account check or personal tolerance resolves it. Finally, write the action, reason and timestamp in one sentence. This compact audit is deliberately repeatable: it reduces missed details without pretending that warehouse photography can certify future performance. Review the saved note once more before parcel submission, because combining items or changing packaging can introduce new questions that were not part of the original item-level QC decision.

Warehouse evidence record

Use one row per item and keep the original files. A short, chronological record makes the decision auditable and gives support enough context without exposing unrelated personal information.

  • Issue class
  • Exact location
  • Overview image
  • Detail and second angle
  • Scale reference
  • Expected-parts source
  • Requested resolution and time

Final review before parcel submission

Confirm that the photographed item matches the paid order, every material uncertainty has either been resolved or knowingly accepted, and the decision is recorded. Then review the parcel contents, packaging choices, destination, route restrictions, address and amount shown at checkout. A QC pass concerns the visible item evidence; it does not guarantee future performance, customs treatment, delivery speed or final shipping cost.

If one remaining image could change the action, request that exact view. If the evidence already supports a return or exchange and the current workflow allows it, act before the displayed deadline. If you ship, record why the known condition and unknowns are acceptable.
