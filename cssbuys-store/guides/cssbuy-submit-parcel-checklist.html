How to Submit a CSSBuy Parcel: A Pre-Shipping Checklist

Open My intl order in your CSSBuy User Center, select the warehouse items you want to ship, and enter your delivery details. Before paying the shipping deposit, check the photos, the packing instructions and the route offered for that combination of items. Here is how to make those decisions, using a pair of shoes and a hoodie as a running example.

Start with the items CSSBuy has received

Open the record for each item you intend to select. Match its order reference, quantity and chosen option to what you bought. A single product listing might cover a dozen colours. The photograph on the listing can help identify the model, but the warehouse photographs show which colour and size arrived. If you bought two of one item, count the units before adding the order to the parcel. A seller's domestic delivery notification and a warehouse item ready to select are two different milestones.

For the shoes, read the size on both labels and compare the pair. For the hoodie, line up the photographed label and a useful measurement with the seller's size chart. If the chart gives a flat chest width, compare it with another flat width. A photo that cuts off the end of the ruler leaves the question open. Ask for the missing view while the item is in the warehouse. Keep an unresolved item out of the parcel and check any return deadline shown for that particular order before you decide to wait for another delivery.

Choose what to consolidate

CSSBuy offers consolidation so several warehouse items can travel in one outer parcel. Write down the available route, estimated freight and packing services for the combined selection. Then check the same fields for the alternatives you would actually use. Two parcels may each incur a starting charge, while a larger combined box can attract a higher billable weight. You need both quotes to see which difference matters for your items and destination.

Imagine the hoodie has arrived but you are waiting for the seller to exchange one shoe. Shipping the hoodie alone means paying for a second parcel later. Waiting could make sense if the exchange fits the warehouse timing and you have no deadline for the hoodie. If another item in the same order has a battery, check which routes accept that combination. A route available for a garment alone may disappear when the other item is selected. Decide which items and services to compare before relying on a displayed price.

For a fair comparison, keep the destination, service level and packing assumptions the same in both estimates. If you change the shipping line at the same time as you remove a box, you cannot tell which decision changed the price.

Give the warehouse a packing instruction it can follow

With a shoebox, decide what you want to receive. A collector who needs the original box should request that it travel inside the shipping carton and check the reinforcement options shown for the parcel. If you only need the shoes, ask whether the warehouse can remove that specific retail box while protecting the shoes from rubbing or being crushed. Identify the order reference in the note. A general instruction to remove all boxes could also discard packaging you wanted to keep for another item.

Describe the result in plain language. For example, “For the shoes in order A, remove the retail box if available and keep the pair together. Keep the hoodie in its protective bag.” Adjust those instructions to the options in your account. The hoodie may help fill space, but a hard buckle, exposed zipper or heavy item packed against a shoe can mark the surface. When shape matters, ask what protection the warehouse can provide. Compare any service charge and the resulting packed dimensions before choosing.

Use the packed size to check a 2 kg estimate

Enter the destination and parcel details in the official shipping calculator. Use the weight units shown on screen. When it asks for grams, a 2 kg parcel is 2,000 g. Enter the outer length, width and height you expect after packing. Product measurements from a size chart describe the item and cannot stand in for those dimensions. Save the route name and date alongside the estimate so you can repeat the same comparison when the warehouse has measured the carton.

For an illustration, suppose the shoes and hoodie together weigh 2 kg packed. A box measuring 40 × 30 × 20 cm occupies 24,000 cubic centimetres. A smaller outer carton measuring 35 × 25 × 18 cm occupies 15,750. Even if both briefly show the same scale weight, the smaller carton can make a difference on a route priced by volume. Use the volumetric divisor, minimum charge and rounding rule published for the route on your screen. A route priced by actual weight could give a different answer. Neither box size alone tells you a price.

If your cheapest option depends on staying just below a size or weight limit, check the estimate with slightly larger dimensions too. This shows whether a little padding would move the parcel to a different price band or make the route unavailable. You can then decide whether to accept the next eligible option or change the packing.

Check the recipient, route and total before paying

Read the recipient details as they would appear on a label. Confirm the destination country, postal code, street number, building or apartment, and the name used for delivery. Check that the contact method is one you can actually monitor. An address saved for a previous parcel can look familiar even when it belongs to a different recipient. Compare these fields with the current order before confirming the route.

Read the route's conditions for the selected items, including size limits, tracking coverage and the delivery estimate. If you add reinforcement or insurance, look at the service description and charge in the account. Save the item selection, packing note, route and total together. CSSBuy calculates the international shipping deposit from the destination, shipping method and estimated weight. The final freight calculation uses the parcel size and weight verified by the shipping company. Record the initial deposit separately from the product purchase and domestic delivery so a later account adjustment can be matched to the right payment.

Follow the parcel after submission

Once you have submitted, look for the parcel record before starting another submission or payment. Match the included order references to your list and keep the parcel reference beside them. If the record shows a packing question, read it while the warehouse can still act on your instructions. If you spot a wrong address or an omitted item, send a note against that parcel and ask whether the requested change is still possible at its current stage.

When the parcel has been sent out, open My Parcel in the User Center and use the tracking information attached to that shipment. In the shoes-and-hoodie example, you may have two seller order references and one international tracking record. Save that record for the delivery journey and keep the incoming seller numbers with the individual orders. At delivery, check the outer carton and unpack against your saved list. If something is missing or damaged, record which item is affected and keep the relevant packing and photos with the parcel reference when contacting support.
