Hoobuy Total Cost Guide: Product, Payment and Shipping Fees

Published: November 6, 2026

A Hoobuy total-cost estimate starts with the exact product option and ends with the cost of receiving the parcel. The price on a spreadsheet is only one part. Keep the item price, domestic delivery, payment costs, optional services and international shipping separate. Add destination charges only from information that applies to your own shipment. A number that has not been confirmed should remain unknown, rather than silently becoming zero.

This guide explains how to build that budget and reconcile later changes. It does not publish a universal Hoobuy service-fee percentage, payment rate or shipping price: those figures need to be checked in the live account and route quotation. The examples below are invented arithmetic examples, clearly labelled, so you can replace every amount with your own confirmed values.

1. Start with one item, one option and one currency

Open the original listing and select the intended size, colour and quantity. A low price on a product card may refer to another option, a minimum quantity or an older listing. Record the amount and currency after selecting your option. Keep the seller URL and a dated screenshot together. This gives you a useful reference if the agent later asks you to approve a difference.

Use one currency for the working budget. If the seller charges in CNY and your payment account uses another currency, preserve both amounts instead of replacing the original. Record the conversion rate shown for the actual payment, including its date. A search-engine exchange-rate estimate is useful for planning, but it does not establish what your bank or payment provider will charge.

2. Separate the costs by stage

Budget lineEvidence to saveWhen to confirm
Selected productsVariant, quantity and item subtotalBefore submitting the purchase
Domestic deliverySeller delivery amount and package countBefore approving the order total
Service or payment chargesCheckout fee lines and final payment amountAt payment
Optional warehouse servicesSelected service and displayed chargeBefore requesting the service
International shippingRoute, packed weight, dimensions and quoteBefore parcel payment
Destination chargesApplicable carrier or official assessmentWhen the shipment details are known

Do not count a charge twice. If a checkout total already includes domestic delivery, either record that inclusive total or separate its components, but do not add the delivery line again. Apply the same rule to a payment fee included in the amount charged to your card. When the description is unclear, ask what the quoted amount includes before treating it as the final total.

3. Work through an illustrative budget

Suppose two selected products cost CNY 180 and CNY 120. Assume domestic delivery is CNY 12, an optional service costs CNY 8, the separately displayed payment cost is CNY 10, and the international shipping estimate is CNY 190. These are example inputs, not Hoobuy prices or a quote for any destination. The known subtotal is CNY 520: 180 + 120 + 12 + 8 + 10 + 190.

If the final shipping amount becomes CNY 220, the subtotal becomes CNY 550. The increase is CNY 30, not a second CNY 220 payment to add on top of the estimate in your cost ledger. Keep an estimated column and a final column so the replacement is explicit. Any unconfirmed destination charge remains outside this subtotal and should be marked as pending.

The purpose of this example is to make the missing information visible. A budget of CNY 520 with destination costs unconfirmed is different from an all-inclusive promise of CNY 520. You can reserve your own contingency for uncertainty, but identify it as your planning buffer. It is not an additional platform fee and should not be presented as one.

4. Check why the shipping estimate changes

Before warehouse packing, the total weight and outer dimensions may be estimates. Packaging can change both. A bulky but light item may produce a different chargeable weight from a compact item with the same scale weight. Compare the selected route's actual rules, including its volume divisor, minimum charge, rounding and eligible item types. Do not assume that every route prices only the physical kilograms.

For a purely mathematical example, a 40 × 30 × 25 cm parcel has a volume of 30,000 cubic centimetres. A divisor of 6,000 gives 5 kg of volumetric weight; a divisor of 5,000 gives 6 kg. Neither divisor is a promise about your route. Use the live route rule, then check whether its price also includes additional services or surcharges. Record the dimensions before comparing two estimates.

5. Compare payment choices using the amount actually charged

If several payment methods are offered, compare the same order and currency at the same time. Record the order amount, any separately displayed payment cost, the exchange conversion and the final amount payable. A low advertised percentage is not enough to compare methods when conversion and fixed charges differ. Stop before confirming a payment if a line cannot be explained from the checkout screen.

A wallet top-up and a purchase paid from that wallet are related movements, not necessarily two separate purchase costs. Record money entering the wallet as funding, then record the order payment against the relevant order. For a refund, distinguish a requested refund, a wallet credit and money returned to the original payment method. Count the settled amount rather than assuming that every pending credit is already available.

6. Decide whether an optional service is useful

Choose additional photographs, measurements, packing or other services because they answer a specific question or protect a specific item. Write the question first. For example, a flat chest measurement may help resolve a size uncertainty, while another general photograph may not. Check the current availability and price in the item record. This guide does not assume that any particular service is free or offered for every item.

Compare the service cost with the decision it enables. If extra evidence could prevent sending the wrong size internationally, it may be useful before parcel submission. If you already have the needed measurement, a duplicate request may add no value. Keep the service confirmation with the item ID so it does not become an unexplained expense in a combined parcel budget.

7. Reconcile adjustments without losing the original quote

Save the initial order total, any requested adjustment, your response and the final settled amount. When an outstanding balance appears, identify whether it belongs to product purchase, domestic delivery or parcel shipping. These are different stages and may use different records. Compare the requested difference with the underlying change instead of sending another payment just because a status label looks urgent.

Before paying for a parcel, reconcile the item list, optional services, packed measurements, selected route and amount due. After shipment, update the final column when the account records a settlement or refund. Keep the original estimate for comparison. This lets you see whether the main uncertainty came from the product option, packing, currency conversion or the route itself.

Questions to answer before you pay

Does the spreadsheet price include shipping?

Do not assume so. Confirm the selected item price, seller delivery amount and international parcel quote separately. A product card is a research lead, not an all-inclusive checkout.

Can I use one fixed Hoobuy fee percentage?

Use the fee lines displayed for your actual transaction. A single percentage cannot represent every payment method, optional service, route or later adjustment.

Should unknown charges be entered as zero?

No. Mark them as unconfirmed and identify the source needed to resolve them. Zero means a cost has been confirmed as absent; unknown means the budget is incomplete.

For the next order, complete the product and domestic-delivery lines first, then save a current shipping estimate with its inputs. Review the unresolved fields before committing to a large purchase. A useful total-cost budget is an auditable record of your own transaction, with assumptions visible and later changes traceable.
