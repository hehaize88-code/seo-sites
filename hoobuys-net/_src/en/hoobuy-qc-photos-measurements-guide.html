Hoobuy QC Photos: Find, Read and Request Measurements

Published: October 12, 2026

HooBuys.net is an independent editorial website, not Hoobuy. Check current account messages, service options and official terms before making a purchase or shipping decision.

Hoobuy QC photos are most useful when you know which decision each image should support. A photograph can help confirm a visible label, quantity, colour, measurement or condition. It cannot promise authenticity, hidden material quality, long-term durability or how an item will fit your body. Begin by matching the photos to the exact warehouse item, then ask only for the missing evidence that could change your next action.

This is an independent inspection method, not a description of guaranteed free services. Photo locations, available requests and charges can change. Check the current item record and the options shown in your account before requesting additional work. Never assume that a photo package seen in another buyer's account is included in yours.

Find the correct item before judging the images

Open your order and its connected warehouse or stored-item record. Look for the photos attached to that item rather than relying on a generic product picture. The exact interface wording may vary. If the record has no images, establish whether the item has actually completed intake and whether a photo service is available. A seller dispatch notice or domestic delivery scan does not itself mean the warehouse has photographed your item.

Compare the source listing, selected variant, quantity and identifiers. An agent order ID, warehouse item ID and parcel ID refer to different records. Keep their relationship visible using the identifier guide. Where one order arrives in several packages, confirm which photographed item belongs to each package before reporting a missing piece.

Download or save the images you need together with their date and item reference. Preserve an unedited copy before making annotations. Publicly sharing a screenshot can expose names, addresses, tracking numbers or account information; crop or redact those details in any copy you share while retaining the original privately for support.

Checking identity first prevents wasted effort on a detailed measurement of the wrong item. Checking quantity early matters when sets, pairs or accessories are involved. Once those basics match, inspect the areas that matter for the specific category rather than applying an identical checklist to every product.

Read clothing measurements carefully

For a T-shirt or hoodie, decide which measurements you need: chest width, garment length, sleeve length or shoulder width. Use a garment that already fits as your comparison and measure it in the same way. If your reference chest width is 54 cm laid flat and the seller's chart states 108 cm circumference, those values may describe the same nominal dimension, but only if the methods match. Do not compare 54 directly with 108 as though both were flat widths.

A useful measurement photograph shows the whole relevant section, where the tape starts, where it ends and the numbers clearly enough to read. Fabric should lie naturally without unexplained stretching or folds. A curved tape, hidden zero point or angled camera can make an apparently precise number unreliable. Ask for a clearer method before treating a small difference as conclusive.

Record the observed value and any uncertainty separately. For example, “chest approximately 54 cm, tape start partly hidden” is more useful than “size correct.” Decide your acceptable measurement range before reviewing the result, based on your own garment and preferences. This guide does not supply one universal tolerance for every fabric or clothing type.

Adapt the check to shoes and accessories

For shoes, inspect both shoes, the visible size labels, sole pattern, left-right consistency and any obvious damage. If length information matters, specify what should be measured. The outside sole length does not equal internal usable length, and an insole may not be removable. Ask whether the requested method is available rather than instructing someone to damage or dismantle the item.

For bags, check overall dimensions, handles or straps, closures, visible seams and included accessories. For items with rigid parts, check obvious deformation and whether protection will be needed during shipping. These observations help with acceptance and packaging decisions, but a photograph cannot demonstrate load capacity or extended use.

Keep the seller's reference beside your item photos. Differences in camera angle and lighting can alter the appearance of colour and shape. Where a colour difference is decisive, request a neutral, well-lit image if that option exists. Describe the uncertainty instead of assuming either that every difference is a defect or that every difference is caused by lighting.

Write one precise request for missing evidence

A useful request identifies the item, the exact view or measurement, the method and the reason it affects your decision. For example: “For this warehouse item, please show the chest width laid flat from underarm to underarm, with both tape endpoints visible. I need to compare it with my reference garment before deciding whether to keep it.” Include the actual item reference only in the private support or service request.

For a visible concern, identify the area rather than asking for “better QC.” A request for a close view of the left cuff seam or the heel of the right shoe is actionable. If several details are missing, combine compatible requests into one clear list and check any displayed charge before submitting it. Repeated broad requests can create more images without resolving the original question.

Save the request wording and response with the item record. If a requested check cannot be performed, keep that limitation visible and decide whether the remaining uncertainty is acceptable. Silence, an unavailable service or an unrelated image should not be recorded as a passed inspection.

Choose between acceptance, clarification and a return enquiry

Accept the visible evidence only when identity, quantity and your decision-critical checks are resolved. If a missing image could change the result, request clarification before packing. If the available evidence shows a mismatch or unacceptable condition, preserve the original order details and photos, then review the current return or exchange options. Availability, eligibility and deadlines depend on the actual transaction and current terms.

The returns and exchanges guide explains how to organise that evidence. Avoid assuming that a warehouse rejection automatically creates a refund, or that a photo request pauses a deadline. If the account displays a time limit, record it and ask for clarification while that option is still available.

Carry the decision into the parcel stage

Item QC and parcel packing answer different questions. Accepting a garment's visible condition does not establish that the final box has suitable protection or the dimensions used for a shipping quote. Record which items are accepted, which remain unresolved and which must be excluded from the parcel. Then write packaging instructions that match the accepted contents.

Use the shipping preparation guide to collect the final weight and outer dimensions, and the packaging guide to compare protection choices. Removing packaging solely to reduce volume can create a different risk. The decision should follow the item's shape and protection needs, not a generic rule applied to every order.

Keep an inspection record you can explain

A compact record contains the item reference, selected option, photo date, observations, unresolved questions, request history and final decision. Link it to the private order-tracking worksheet. Use factual observations such as “two pieces visible” or “label shows size M,” and keep conclusions such as “acceptable for my use” separate.

This separation makes later support requests clearer. It also prevents a reference photo from becoming an unsupported claim that an entire seller or product line has been verified. The useful result of QC is a documented decision about the item in front of you, with its limits understood before international shipping makes correction more complicated.
