CSSBuy Product Links: Find, Verify and Plan Your Order

CSSBuys Pro Editorial · Updated 4 November 2026

A CSSBuy product link is a starting point for an order, not a guarantee that a particular size, colour or version is available. W2C means “where to cop”: in practice, it is the original listing or seller route that lets you identify what you intend to buy. A useful link should let you check the marketplace, item, seller and selected variation before money is committed.

CSSBuy’s official buying guide describes copying a product link into its search field, choosing the variation and paying for the goods and Chinese domestic delivery. Warehouse inspection and international shipping follow later. This independent guide helps you organise those decisions; product availability, purchasing and account support remain with the seller and CSSBuy.

1. Identify the original listing

Open the link and look beyond the main photograph. Record the marketplace and the item identifier shown in the address or listing, where available. Check the seller or shop name, the product description and the selectable options. Two links with similar photographs can refer to different sellers, materials, quantities or versions. A community nickname is useful for finding an item but is not a specification.

Keep the original link alongside any CSSBuy link you use. This makes it easier to recognise a changed listing or explain a problem to support. If a shortened address redirects, inspect the final product page before proceeding. Do not submit passwords, payment details or account verification codes to a page merely because it resembles a familiar shopping service.

2. Confirm size, colour and quantity

Select the exact variation before comparing prices. A low headline price may belong to a different option or quantity band. Check whether a displayed quantity describes individual pieces, pairs, sets or a minimum purchase. CSSBuy’s buying guide specifically notes that many 1688 suppliers have minimum quantities, so a single-unit budget should not be assumed to work for every listing.

For clothing, compare the seller’s measurement method with a garment you already own. For shoes, distinguish the size selected in the order from a size mentioned only in an image. Save a screenshot showing the intended option, quantity and price. If the option name is ambiguous, request clarification before payment; a note cannot reliably correct a different variation selected in the order itself.

3. Keep a compact product record

Use one row per selected item, not one row for an entire shop. Keep confirmed values separate from estimates and leave unknown values visibly blank. This is particularly useful when you collect links over several days or combine items bought at different times. The spreadsheet guide explains how to organise the wider list.

Update the row when the order changes. If the seller proposes a replacement, record it as a new decision rather than leaving the original description untouched. Keep private order identifiers in your own record; remove them from a publicly shared spreadsheet when they expose account information.

4. Import the link and review the order

Paste the original product URL into CSSBuy’s own search field and compare the imported product with the original listing. Confirm the option labels, quantity and domestic delivery cost again. A successful import proves that a page was recognised; it does not prove stock, authenticity, delivery eligibility or that every detail was transferred correctly.

If automatic import does not work, CSSBuy’s official guide points to Expert Buy. Its public form requests the item URL or information, title, images, price, domestic shipping and quantity. Provide a clear, factual specification and ask how an unclear seller instruction will be handled. Manual purchasing is not a guarantee that an unavailable product can be bought.

5. Handle missing or changed links

A failed link can mean a removed listing, an unavailable variation, a changed address or a temporary access problem. Start with the original URL and seller information you saved. Check whether the listing itself still exists before trying to reconstruct an agent link. Do not assume that changing an item number or substituting a similar photograph points to the same goods.

If you find an alternative, compare it as a separate listing: seller, version, measurements, price, return conditions and shipping attributes. Keep the old entry marked unavailable until its status is clarified. For an existing paid order, ask through the relevant order record rather than placing a duplicate order simply because the public link no longer opens.

6. Turn the listing into a QC checklist

When the item reaches the warehouse, compare the received item with the saved variation and specifications. Seller photographs show the offer; warehouse QC photographs show the received item at the time photographed. Check identity and quantity first, then colour, measurements, visible damage and included components. Use the QC photo checklist to keep the review consistent.

A photograph cannot establish every hidden property, battery condition or long-term performance. If one missing view prevents a decision, request that specific view and confirm any fee. Resolve questions promptly while return options can still be checked. The returns and refunds guide separates a seller return from a withdrawal of account balance.

7. Check shipping before building a larger basket

Identify bulky packaging, fragile parts, liquids, batteries or other relevant attributes before combining several finds. A correct item can still be unsuitable for a particular shipping route. Do not remove or change an accurate product description merely to make more routes appear. Ask CSSBuy about the exact item and destination if acceptance is unclear.

Keep product cost and international freight separate. Use realistic packed dimensions and weight, not just a seller’s garment measurements, when estimating a parcel. The shipping calculator guide helps you compare consistent inputs. A cheap item may be poor value when its packaging raises the chargeable weight of the whole parcel.

8. A practical decision example

Imagine finding a jacket through a shared list. The photograph looks right, but the listing has a lightweight and an insulated version. First record which version you want, its measurements and quantity. Confirm whether the quoted price applies to that selection. At warehouse arrival, compare the label and measurements with your saved record before deciding whether to keep it.

Only after that review should you decide about packaging and international delivery. If the jacket needs a different carton, update the parcel estimate instead of keeping the original shipping figure. This example is a workflow illustration, not a product recommendation, a live stock check or a quoted cost.

Frequently asked questions

Does a working CSSBuy link mean the item is in stock?

No. Confirm the selected variation and the seller’s current availability. Importing a listing and completing a purchase are different stages.

Can I use the same photos to compare different sellers?

They can help identify a style, but identical pictures do not prove identical goods. Compare the listing details and review the item actually received.

Should I pay international shipping when I first choose the item?

The official workflow separates purchasing from later parcel dispatch. Budget for both stages and check the account’s current payment instructions.

What should I save before a listing disappears?

Keep the source address, seller, item identifier, exact variation, price, date and relevant screenshots. These records help explain an order; they do not replace the seller’s current terms.
