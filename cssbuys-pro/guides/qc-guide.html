QC Photo Guide

A guide to QC photo review before shipment.

QC content helps buyers slow down before paying international shipping. A product route may be found, but warehouse photos decide whether the item is acceptable.

CSSBuy public guidance says quality inspection is carried out after warehouse arrival and photos are taken for users to view. The homepage also mentions unlimited QC images.

Many users ask about extra photos and video. I did not find a verified public extra-photo price or video-QC price on the reviewed pages, so a responsible article should not invent those costs.

A good QC checklist varies by item. Shoes need shape and sole checks. Hoodies need fabric, print and tag checks. Bags need hardware and lining checks. Electronics need package and surface-condition checks.

QC is connected to return timing. CSSBuy says returns can be helped when conditions are met, including item being in warehouse less than 7 days and return shipping fees being paid.

Good QC does not mean perfection. It is a reasonable decision based on product type, price, defect risk and buyer tolerance.

Independent-site disclaimer: this site does not take QC photos, sell extra-photo services, shoot videos or process returns.

Read the complete image set in a fixed order

Begin with identity: does the received item match the ordered color, size and variation? Then check shape and construction, followed by category-specific details. Finish with packaging and any measurement image. Using the same order prevents a dramatic close-up from distracting you from a more basic mismatch. Save the warehouse item ID and the date of review so the decision can be traced later.

For footwear, compare the left and right shoe, toe shape, heel alignment, outsole, stitching and size tag. For clothing, check the front, back, print or embroidery placement, seams, labels and measurements relevant to fit. For bags and accessories, check symmetry, hardware finish, closures, lining and included pieces. Electronics require extra caution: a surface photo can show damage or the package contents, but it cannot prove battery health, long-term performance or route eligibility.

Use measurements, not visual guesses

Perspective can make an item look wider or narrower. If fit depends on a number, ask for or use a photo that clearly shows the ruler position and both endpoints. Compare the measurement with the seller’s stated method because chest width, circumference and garment length may be measured differently. Record tolerance before reviewing the image; changing the tolerance after seeing the result makes the decision inconsistent.

Classify the finding before acting

Use four labels: confirmed match, cosmetic issue, functional risk and unresolved question. A loose thread and a broken closure should not share the same severity. For an unresolved question, request one view that would change the decision. If the image cannot answer it, do not keep ordering more photos without a clear purpose.

Make the accept-or-return decision while the relevant return conditions can still be checked. Preserve screenshots of the selected variation and the warehouse images. If return assistance is requested, describe the mismatch factually and avoid conclusions that the images cannot support. QC reduces avoidable surprises; it does not guarantee authenticity, hidden construction or future durability.

Is shipping price fixed?

No. Final cost depends on destination, route, actual weight, parcel volume, product attributes and current line restrictions.

What does the CSSBuy calculator check?

It asks for product weight, volume, commodity attributes and destination, then estimates available shipping methods, transit time and shipping fee.

What is volumetric weight?

Volumetric weight is chargeable weight based on parcel dimensions. Many freight lines charge by whichever is larger: actual weight or volume weight.
