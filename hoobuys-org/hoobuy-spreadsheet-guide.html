If you need another product, use the search form with an item type and a useful attribute. Try a garment cut, a required bag dimension or a construction detail that distinguishes the candidates. Keep the result's source URL and selected option. A shared image or familiar title alone is not enough to identify the item you will receive.

Use one row for one selected option

A practical spreadsheet should make the next action clear. Give each candidate its own row, preserving the original marketplace link and item ID. Record colour, size, version and quantity separately. If you are deciding between two sizes, keep two candidate options visible until you resolve the measurement question.

Verify Taobao, 1688 and Weidian links

Follow the explicit source link and check the final marketplace domain, product identifier and seller. A discovery page can use a different numeric ID from the marketplace listing it references. Do not move digits from one website's URL into another and assume they identify the same product.

Match the selected option after any redirect. For example, a listing might remain available while the colour and size in a shared sheet have disappeared. In that case, keep the original row and mark the option unavailable. Use the working-link verification guide for normal checks and the broken-link guide when a destination or import fails.

Compare sizes with a reference you understand

For clothing, measure a similar garment you already wear using the same endpoints and units as the seller's chart. Flat chest width, body circumference and a recommended fit range describe different things. Keep the measurement method beside the number. A size label such as M or L is not a universal measurement.

For trousers, consider rise and inseam as well as the waist. For shoes, record the seller's sizing method and the intended label. For bags, compare usable capacity and opening dimensions with the things you plan to carry. The measurement workflow helps you avoid comparing incompatible figures.

A candidate with missing fit evidence should remain pending. Do not replace a missing measurement with a guess based on a model photograph. The women's clothing and accessories guide gives category-specific checks for tops, dresses, trousers, bags and belts.

Separate seller pictures from your order's QC

Seller photographs show the advertised design. Historical warehouse photos may show a previous item. Your order's QC evidence should be tied to the actual received item and selected option. Keep these three kinds of images labelled separately so a browsing image cannot accidentally become an approval record.

When warehouse evidence is available, check visible labels, colour, condition and relevant measurements. Ask for a specific view when an important point is unclear: for example, the whole ruler across a flat chest measurement, both shoe size labels or the closure of a bag. Use the QC photo checklist to plan those requests.

Photos have limits. They cannot prove long-term durability, comfort, exact material composition or colour under every light. A missing view is an unanswered question, not an automatic pass. Keep that question visible before moving the item into a shipment plan.

Record the selected price and the whole purchase cost

Save the selected option's displayed price, currency and check date. Separate it from a catalogue headline price and from any personal currency conversion. Add domestic delivery and other confirmed charges only when you have evidence for the relevant order or service. Leave unknown amounts pending.

Use the budget finds comparison to narrow a shortlist by requirement and complete cost. A lower product price is not automatically the cheaper purchase if the item needs different packing or changes the shipment. The total-cost guide provides a detailed structure for keeping estimates and paid amounts apart.

Move approved items into a dated parcel plan

Once important order questions are resolved, list the items you intend to ship together. Record destination, actual contents and packaging requirements. Item weights are useful early information, but the final parcel also includes packaging. Keep packed dimensions and packed weight in the parcel record instead of treating them as product attributes.

Compare estimates using consistent inputs. If you add a jacket or decide to keep a shoe box, update the parcel scenario. The shipping calculator guide explains actual and volumetric weight with worked arithmetic. It does not provide live transport prices or guarantee that a route accepts your parcel.

Use simple statuses instead of a vague verified label

Choose statuses that describe what you actually know: discovered, needs measurement, ready to consider, ordered, awaiting QC, needs clarification, reviewed or unavailable. A link can work while its price, stock or fit remains uncertain. Calling the whole row verified hides those differences.

Add one next action to every pending row. That might be checking an inseam, confirming the selected version or reviewing a warehouse label. Remove duplicate candidates when they add no useful choice. Keep a replaced row in an archive rather than transferring old evidence to a new listing.

Walk through a first shortlist

Choose one item you need and two candidates. Open both destinations, save the exact options and compare their measurement methods. Recheck selected prices and write down what remains unknown. Resolve the critical questions before choosing a candidate. This small exercise is more useful than saving a large sheet that you cannot verify.

After ordering, add the order reference to the chosen row. Review the received item using its own QC evidence, then create a parcel plan only when the relevant questions are answered. If something changes, update that stage of the record. A useful spreadsheet preserves the connection between discovery, purchase and shipment without pretending every stage is already complete.
