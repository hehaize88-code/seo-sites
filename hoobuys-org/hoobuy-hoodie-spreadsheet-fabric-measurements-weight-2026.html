HooBuy Hoodie Spreadsheet: Fabric, Measurements and Weight

Published and fact-checked: September 5, 2026

Independent-site disclosure: hoobuys.org is not HooBuy. A saved product page is discovery evidence, not proof of current stock, composition, fit, quality or final shipping cost. Reopen the exact listing and selected option before ordering.

A useful HooBuy hoodie spreadsheet does more than collect attractive links. Hoodies that share a similar front image may differ in cut, fabric claim, lining, print method, option naming, size-chart method, packaging and weight. If those facts live in one notes cell, comparison becomes guesswork.

The solution is a field-level record that keeps seller claims, measurements, later warehouse evidence and editor decisions separate. This guide defines the minimum columns, shows how to handle missing data and prevents one listing’s details from being copied onto another.

Begin with identity, not style

Create a row only after opening the final destination page. Save the original source URL, final URL after redirects, marketplace, item identifier, seller or shop identifier, exact displayed title and verification date. Add a short visual fingerprint such as “pullover, kangaroo pocket, small chest print” only for matching—not as a substitute for the source ID.

Follow the seller-page checklist before the row enters a shortlist. If the destination changes seller, product type or option set, keep the old URL in a history field and create a new candidate. Do not silently overwrite identity.

Store the exact option grammar

Hoodie listings can combine color, print, lining, thickness, fit or version labels. Save each selector separately and copy its text exactly. “Black / L” is insufficient if another selector distinguishes a fleece-lined edition from an unlined one. Record quantity and any seller instructions that affect the exact unit.

Add a normalized comparison label in a separate editorial column only after preserving the source text. For example, a source label may be normalized to “dark grey” for filtering, but the order note must retain the original characters or wording. Normalization supports search; it must never replace the purchasable option.

Split fabric information by source

A product page may state a fiber name, percentage, fabric weight, lining or seasonal description. Put each in its own seller-claim field and retain the date. Do not merge “cotton,” “cotton blend,” “heavyweight” and “fleece” into one confidence score. They describe different things, and words such as heavyweight or premium may have no published test method.

Add columns for claim source, exact wording and evidence state. Use values such as seller-stated, label-visible, warehouse-visible or unknown. Product photography can show knit structure, fleece-like lining or surface finish, but it cannot prove fiber percentage, softness, warmth, shrinkage or durability.

Source rule: a fabric claim stays a claim until stronger evidence supports it. Repeating the same seller statement in several images does not create independent confirmation.

Make every measurement comparable

Never save a number without its garment point, method, unit and size. “Chest 60” could mean a half-chest width, full circumference, body measurement or a translated label. The spreadsheet should identify the endpoint pair and whether the garment is laid flat, stretched or relaxed.

Keep the complete seller size chart or a traceable transcription, not just the row you hope to order. Record whether a tolerance is actually stated. If it is not, leave the tolerance blank. Do not invent a generic manufacturing allowance.

Compare to a reference garment correctly

A buyer’s familiar hoodie is a better comparison than body height alone, but only when measured by the same method. Create reference-garment fields for size label, chest width, body length, sleeve method, desired change and measurement date. Use the same unit and tape path as the listing chart.

Measure on a flat surface without pulling the fabric. A tape is useful only when its zero point, path and endpoints are visible and repeatable. Add a method-match flag: yes, no or unclear. If methods do not match, show the difference as unresolved rather than computing false precision.

Record weight without pretending it is universal

Hoodie weight can vary by size, fabric, lining, print, hardware and retail packaging. Create separate fields for seller-stated item weight, selected-size applicability, warehouse item weight, packed item allocation and source date. Never copy one size’s weight across every variant.

Use provenance labels such as estimate, seller-stated, platform-displayed, warehouse-measured or derived. A number without provenance should not feed a parcel-cost model. Even a warehouse item weight may not equal the eventual share of the international parcel because outer packaging and consolidation add mass and volume.

Connect discovery fields to later QC

The spreadsheet should define what later evidence needs to resolve, not claim that a future service will provide a fixed number of photos. Add QC request fields for exact ordered variant, full front and back identity, size tag, defined measurement endpoints, print placement, lining view and any critical construction detail.

Write one decision question per request. “Confirm body length from the shoulder high point to the hem” is testable. “Please check quality” is not. Keep request status, received evidence and decision in separate columns so the original requirement remains visible.

Use an evidence-state vocabulary

  • Confirmed: the exact selected listing state or later evidence supports the field.
  • Seller-stated: the source page makes the claim, but it is not independently verified.
  • Derived: the value was calculated from preserved inputs and formula.
  • Conflicting: two sources disagree and both remain in the record.
  • Unknown: no adequate evidence exists.
  • Stale: the source is no longer current enough for the intended decision.

Do not use blank cells for all six states. A blank can mean forgotten, unavailable or not applicable. Explicit states make filtering honest.

Worked comparison: two similar black hoodies

Candidate A and Candidate B use near-identical front images. A lists separate lined and unlined variants, provides a size chart with half-chest width, and states an item-weight estimate without naming the applicable size. B has only color and size selectors, describes the fabric as heavyweight, and uses a chart labeled as circumference.

The spreadsheet does not declare A better. It records that A has clearer option identity but unresolved weight applicability. B has a marketing fabric claim and a measurement method that cannot be directly compared to A until the circumference definition is confirmed. The next action is different for each row: verify A’s size-specific weight relevance and clarify B’s chart method.

Run a pre-order completeness gate

  1. Final product and seller identity verified on the current date.
  2. Every selected option copied exactly and separately.
  3. Price recorded only after selecting the intended state.
  4. Fabric wording preserved as a dated seller claim.
  5. Required measurements include units, endpoints and method.
  6. Reference garment uses the same measurement method.
  7. Weight includes source, stage and variant applicability.
  8. QC questions are specific and decision-changing.
  9. Unknowns and conflicts are visible.
  10. A stop condition exists for any critical unresolved field.

Maintain the record after ordering

Freeze the pre-order row, then append order ID, selected options, seller changes, warehouse identity and later measurements. If a listing changes, preserve the earlier capture and add a new verification event. The shoes spreadsheet guide uses the same identity discipline but different category fields; do not reuse shoe-specific packaging or size assumptions for hoodies.

Finish by separating three conclusions: whether the correct hoodie can be ordered, whether later visible evidence matches the order and whether the final parcel fits the buyer’s cost plan. A strong HooBuy hoodie measurement record supports all three without pretending that a link, photograph or weight estimate proves fit and quality on its own.
