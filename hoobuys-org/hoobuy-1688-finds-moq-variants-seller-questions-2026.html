HooBuy 1688 Finds: Compare MOQ, Variants and Seller Questions

Published and fact-checked: August 28, 2026

Independent-site disclosure: hoobuys.org is an independent product-discovery guide and is not HooBuy or 1688. HooBuy's current beginner guide says users can paste links from 1688, search recommended 1688 products, select color, size and quantity when information is captured, and contact a shopping agent during purchase. It does not guarantee that every listing, option or seller request can be processed. Verify the exact live order before payment.

Finding a low displayed price on 1688 is not the same as finding a buyable option. A listing may show a price tied to a quantity tier, a color-size combination may have its own availability, domestic delivery may appear later, and a supplier image may cover several products that are not included together. A useful HooBuy 1688 finds spreadsheet must record the purchase basis, not just the headline amount.

This guide focuses on the decision that comes after discovery: which 1688 candidate can be specified clearly enough for a purchasing agent to order and later verify? The goal is not to predict seller quality. It is to reduce ambiguity about minimum quantity, price tier, variant, included pieces and unresolved questions before money moves.

Start with the final source page

Open the exact 1688 source URL and save it before comparing anything. Remove tracking fragments only if the product identity remains unchanged. Record the supplier or shop name shown, listing title, product or offer identifier and date checked. If HooBuy resolves the link, compare the imported title and options with the source instead of assuming the import is complete.

Use the existing product-link verification workflow when identity is uncertain. This article begins after the source has been linked correctly. It does not repeat dead-link recovery or generic W2C cleaning.

Record MOQ as a rule, not a single number

MOQ means the minimum quantity required for the relevant purchase. The important phrase is “for the relevant purchase.” A supplier may show different conditions by option, assortment or quantity band. Your sheet should never contain only “MOQ: 2” without explaining whether two identical pieces, two mixed colors or two units across selected variants satisfy the live listing.

If the requirement is unclear, mark “question open.” Do not choose a convenient interpretation. HooBuy's beginner guide says shopping agents can contact sellers during purchasing and may ask the buyer for missing information. Turn that handoff into a precise question rather than an instruction to “please check everything.”

Match the price to the exact quantity tier

Create one row per candidate purchase scenario. Suppose a page displays unit price p1 for band q1, p2 for band q2 and p3 for band q3. If your intended quantity belongs to q1, the subtotal is intended quantity multiplied by p1, not by the more attractive p3.

Keep variables until the live page supplies values. Then record currency, tier, quantity, product subtotal and any domestic seller delivery amount shown during the current order. Do not mix the later international parcel estimate into the 1688 listing price. A supplier comparison becomes misleading when one row includes domestic freight and another silently omits it.

Spreadsheet rule: every price needs a basis: exact option, purchasing unit, quantity tier, currency and check date. A number without those fields is not comparable.

Expand variants into unambiguous order lines

Supplier pages can compress many colors, sizes, materials or models into one gallery. Your spreadsheet should expand them. Create a separate order line for each intended option and copy the exact label shown. Add an English note for your own use, but preserve the original label so the purchasing agent has a direct match.

For clothing, record color label, size label, quantity and seller measurement reference. For shoes, record model, colorway, size system and quantity. For hardware or accessories, capture dimensions, finish, connector or included-piece option. Never use the gallery's first image as proof of the selected variant.

HooBuy currently says it can capture information for most products from supported source marketplaces, not every product. If a variant field is missing or distorted after import, do not force the closest visible option. Save the source label and use an order remark or enquiry only where the live workflow offers it.

Ask seller questions that produce a decision

A good question identifies the offer, intended option and evidence needed. “Is this available?” is weaker than “For offer ID [recorded], can two units be ordered as one black size M and one grey size L under the displayed minimum?” The second question can confirm or reject a specific plan.

  • Can the displayed minimum be met by mixing the exact listed variants?
  • Which unit does the quoted price cover: piece, pair, set or pack?
  • Does the selected option include every item visible in the reference image?
  • Is the current variant ready to dispatch or produced after order?
  • Which seller measurement chart applies to the selected option?
  • Is domestic delivery shown for this quantity and warehouse destination?
  • Are custom notes required, and do they change return eligibility?

Do not bundle all questions into a long message if only one uncertainty blocks purchase. Ask the smallest decision-changing question first. Save the answer date and wording; do not promote an old reply into a permanent promise for later orders.

Separate supplier evidence from quality conclusions

A detailed listing can make a product easier to specify, but it does not guarantee received quality. Use seller-page fields as purchasing evidence: the exact option, material claim, dimensions, package contents and dispatch statement. Then use warehouse QC as later visual evidence about the delivered item.

HooBuy's beginner guide says items are inspected and photographed after warehouse arrival and specifically notes that 1688 purchases use a different quality-control standard. Because the public guide does not define a universal photo entitlement or acceptance threshold, write your own decision-critical QC notes before purchase. Examples include exact color, number of pieces, visible model marking, size label and one measurable dimension.

Compare candidates with a completeness score

Score each candidate on five binary fields: verified source identity, clear MOQ rule, matching price tier, unambiguous variant and answered critical question. A candidate with five confirmed fields is easier to order than one with a lower headline price but three unresolved fields.

This is not a seller rating. It measures whether your intended purchase can be described and audited. Keep quality claims, customer reviews and sales signals in separate columns with their own sources. Do not allow a popularity indicator to fill an evidence gap about the exact SKU.

Use a controlled comparison example

Imagine Candidate A shows a lower unit price at a higher quantity tier, while Candidate B has a higher unit price but a clearly buyable single-option quantity. Your need is two pieces in different colors. First check whether A permits mixed variants within the displayed minimum. If not confirmed, calculate A only for a compliant quantity or mark it unresolved. Do not multiply two pieces by the lowest displayed tier.

For each valid scenario, add product subtotal and current domestic delivery if shown. Record the number of unwanted extra units, if any. Then compare option clarity, seller answer and planned QC fields. The cheapest compliant scenario may differ from the cheapest number visible at the top of the page.

Pre-payment 1688 finds checklist

  1. Open and save the exact final source URL.
  2. Match source and imported product identity.
  3. Record the purchasing unit and minimum quantity rule.
  4. Select the quantity tier that actually covers the order.
  5. Expand every intended variant into its own line.
  6. Record current domestic seller delivery separately.
  7. Resolve the one question that could stop the purchase.
  8. Write visible QC priorities before submitting.
  9. Review the final live options, remarks, quantities and payable amount.

A useful 1688 find is orderable, not merely attractive

The best HooBuy 1688 finds record connects four things: the source offer, the valid quantity basis, the exact variants and a dated answer to any decision-critical question. That structure prevents a low tier price, broad gallery or ambiguous package unit from becoming a false promise in the spreadsheet.

Once a candidate is complete, preserve the row through purchase and warehouse receipt. Update it with the actual ordered quantities and QC result rather than overwriting the earlier plan. Product discovery then becomes a traceable workflow, not a collection of links whose meaning disappears when the listing changes.
