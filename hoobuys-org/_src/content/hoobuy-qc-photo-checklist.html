Review HooBuy QC photos against the exact item you ordered: product identity, colour, size, quantity, measurements and visible condition. If a critical detail is missing or unclear, keep the decision open and ask about the evidence or current account options before approving shipment.

A good QC review begins with a record of the intended product. Without that record, a clear photograph can still be difficult to interpret. This checklist turns an open-ended search for flaws into a short set of decisions linked to the listing, selected variant and warehouse item.

Match the warehouse item to the order

Open the order record beside the photos. Compare the item identifier, selected colour or version, size and quantity. Preserve the original option wording when it differs from a translated nickname. If several similar products arrived, do not rely on their position in a gallery to decide which is which.

Start a review row with the order ID, warehouse item ID, date and intended use. Attach or reference the relevant photographs. A useful first status is “identity matched” or “identity unresolved”; that prevents later measurements from being assigned to the wrong item.

Separate seller pictures from received-item evidence

Seller pictures explain what was advertised. Warehouse photos show the surfaces and details actually captured for an item. Other buyers' photos may help you plan what to inspect, but they do not establish the condition of your own purchase. Label each image by source before comparing it.

The official product interface provides a QC Photos area and separates product and shipping information. Its risk notice also distinguishes basic inspection from authentication. Do not turn a photograph into a claim of authenticity, hidden material composition or long-term durability. Those questions require evidence a normal photograph may not provide.

Work in that order. A detailed discussion of stitching is premature if the size label is wrong. Likewise, a photograph that looks generally attractive does not resolve a missing part. Mark a check “not applicable” when it truly does not matter for the item instead of creating unnecessary requests.

Check clothing with compatible measurements

For a hoodie or T-shirt, useful dimensions can include flat chest width, garment length and sleeve length. The exact method matters: shoulder-to-cuff and collar-to-cuff sleeve measurements are different. Compare the same endpoints with a garment you already own and like.

A flat width is not a circumference. If a reference shirt is 56 cm across the chest and the photographed garment is 55 cm across using the same method, the difference is 1 cm in flat width. Whether that difference is acceptable depends on your intended fit and measurement uncertainty, not a universal size conversion.

Inspect print placement, seams and visible fabric condition only where the image is clear enough. Lighting can alter colour and a folded garment can hide its shape. Record those limits instead of treating an unclear image as either a pass or a defect.

Check shoes as a pair

Confirm the selected colour or version and both visible size labels. Examine side profiles, heels and outsoles where provided. Positioning and camera angle can create apparent asymmetry, so compare equivalent views before deciding that the left and right shoes differ.

Keep shoe, insole and box measurements separate. An outsole length does not directly establish usable interior space. A matching label confirms the visible marking but cannot guarantee comfort. The shoe spreadsheet guide explains how to preserve the size basis from discovery through QC.

Check bags, hardware and included pieces

For bags, begin with the exterior dimensions and the included strap, handle or accessory list from the exact order. Review visible hardware alignment, closure position, corners and surface condition. A close-up is useful only if its location on the item is clear.

Do not claim that a closed zipper proves smooth operation or that a metal-looking part establishes its composition. State what the image shows, then identify the remaining question. If function matters to the decision, ask what inspection options are currently available instead of assuming a photo answers it.

Write a request that resolves one uncertainty

“Please check quality” is too broad to explain what evidence you need. A more useful request is: “Please show the size label clearly and measure the flat chest width from underarm to underarm, with the garment laid flat and the tape visible.” Adapt the wording to the platform's available request field.

Include the order or warehouse identifier and the specific surface or measurement. State the unit and endpoints. Do not assume additional photographs, measurements or handling are free, available immediately or included for every item. Check the current account terms before requesting a paid service.

Use an evidence-based review example

Imagine a hoodie ordered in a dark colour and size L. The front photograph looks correct, but the size label is unreadable and the only tape image starts below the underarm. The correct outcome is not an automatic approval or rejection. Identity is partly supported; size and chest measurement remain unresolved.

Record two targeted next actions: obtain a readable label view and a measurement with defined endpoints. If later evidence matches the order and your acceptable reference range, you can close those checks. If the label differs, preserve both the order selection and received label when investigating the available after-sale options.

Keep visible damage separate from photo uncertainty

A dark patch could be a stain, a shadow or a fold. Save the image and describe the location without claiming certainty the picture does not support. Another view under different positioning may resolve the issue more efficiently than a long message listing speculative defects.

For a clearly missing component or wrong option, record the mismatch precisely. Avoid editing or annotating over the only original photo; keep a clean copy and a separate marked reference. That makes the evidence easier to review if the conversation continues.

Record the decision before parcel submission

Use a simple decision field: approved for your intended use, evidence requested, mismatch under review, or not proceeding. Add the date and a short reason. Keep the QC decision separate from the shipping decision because packaging, route eligibility and cost are different questions.

There is no universal return window established by this guide. Check the current order status and the applicable seller and platform options promptly if a problem appears. Do not assume that approval, storage, repacking or international shipment has no effect on the options available later.

Connect QC to the rest of the purchase

Once the evidence is sufficient, copy the approved item identifier into the parcel plan. Preserve any packaging requirements, such as protection for a delicate surface or a box you want retained. Review the shipping calculator guide before treating individual item weights as a finished parcel.

Keep requests and any applicable service costs in the total-cost budget. For future purchases, update the spreadsheet with the measurement method or missing view that mattered most. That improves the next inspection without pretending a previous result guarantees a new batch.
