PikoBuy Currency Conversion: Measure the Real Exchange-Rate Cost

A reference rate is not a receipt. Measure conversion from the checkout, authorization and settled transaction that belong together, then keep explicit fees and timing differences visible.

PikoBuy currency conversion is a transaction question, not a universal rate

Users often ask for “the PikoBuy exchange rate” as if one fixed number explains every payment. A cross-currency purchase can involve the seller's source currency, the currency displayed by PikoBuy, the currency submitted to a payment channel, and the cardholder's settled account currency. The conversion actor and timing may differ between transactions.

PikoBuy's current Terms of Service say users pay the purchase price and domestic logistics, and note that the actual calculation may differ slightly because of exchange-rate fluctuations and settlement times. The terms repeat that qualification for the published shipping-stage platform service-fee calculation. This supports recording conversion and settlement differences; it does not publish a permanent FX rate or a fixed currency markup.

The reliable question is narrower: for this completed transaction, what checkout-currency amount led to what final home-currency amount, and which separately labelled charges were included? Answer that before comparing providers, dates or payment methods.

Collect four records without mixing stages

First save the source amount: the selected product and seller-to-warehouse values before any platform display conversion. Second save the checkout amount: the final value, currency code and labelled components immediately before authorization. Third save the authorized amount shown by the card, bank or wallet while the payment is pending. Fourth save the settled amount after posting.

Calculate the observed exchange rate from matching amounts

When the checkout currency and settled account currency differ, divide the final settled amount by the checkout amount. Both values must describe the same completed payment and use the same inclusion boundary. If the settled amount includes an explicit issuer fee, remove that fee before calculating the conversion rate, then report the fee separately.

Observed rate = settled home-currency amount excluding explicit fees ÷ checkout-currency amount

For the inverse view, divide the checkout amount by the adjusted settled amount. Always write the direction beside the number: “home currency per checkout unit” is not interchangeable with “checkout currency per home unit.” Keep enough decimal places to reproduce the calculation, then round only the displayed result.

If the checkout itself shows both currencies, calculate its implied displayed rate too. That produces two evidence points: the platform-side display and the final payment-side settlement. The difference between them is an observed outcome. It should not automatically be called a hidden PikoBuy fee because the payment channel, network, issuer and timing can contribute.

Compare against a timestamped reference without turning it into a promise

A public reference rate can be a benchmark, but match the currency pair, direction and date as closely as possible. Visa's official calculator describes its result as an indication of the rate a cardholder may receive. Mastercard's official converter says its result is indicative, that an issuer may use another rate or add fees, and that authorization or processing time can determine the applied rate. Those limitations are exactly why the settled statement remains primary evidence.

Calculate an observed comparison only after the transaction posts. Reference-value cost = checkout amount × reference rate. Observed difference = adjusted settled amount − reference-value cost. Observed difference percentage = observed difference ÷ reference-value cost × 100. Label the result as a transaction-specific difference, not an advertised markup.

Do not compare a weekend settlement with a rate captured days later, combine two different currency directions, or use a search-result snippet as the benchmark. Also avoid comparing a rate that excludes fees with a settled total that includes them. A clean comparison tells you what happened; it does not prove who caused every basis point.

Handle pending transactions, partial refunds and account balance separately

A pending authorization is provisional. It may change, disappear or be replaced when the transaction posts. Do not finalize an observed rate from the hold. Record both values if they differ and mark settlement as the closing checkpoint.

For a full or partial refund, preserve the original payment. Add the refunded source amount, returned home-currency amount, refund date and any explicit deduction. Calculate a separate observed refund rate only when you can match the returned amount to a defined source-currency refund. Subtracting the refund from the purchase and calculating one blended rate hides timing and deduction effects.

Account balance introduces another boundary. If a refund lands in platform balance rather than the original payment account, it has not yet become a home-currency cash return. Record the credited amount and currency, then treat any later balance use or withdrawal as a separate event. PikoBuy's public shipping terms also mention account-balance deductions for cash-on-delivery forwarding fees; do not merge such a deduction with an unrelated card conversion.

Worked example: reconcile one payment with neutral values

Suppose a checkout shows 1,000 units in its transaction currency. The final bank record posts 137.50 units in the home currency, including a separately labelled 2.50-unit foreign-transaction fee. These are illustrative units, not a live PikoBuy quote or current market rate.

  1. Remove the explicit 2.50 fee from 137.50, leaving 135.00 for the rate calculation.
  2. Calculate 135.00 ÷ 1,000 = 0.135 home-currency units per checkout unit.
  3. Record 2.50 separately as an issuer-side fee shown on the statement.
  4. If a timestamp-matched reference rate is 0.132, calculate a benchmark value of 132.00.
  5. The observed rate difference is 135.00 − 132.00 = 3.00 home-currency units.
  6. The observed difference percentage is 3.00 ÷ 132.00 × 100, or about 2.27%.

The conclusion is limited: this transaction settled 3.00 units above that benchmark before the explicit issuer fee. The evidence does not by itself assign the difference to PikoBuy, a wallet, a card network, a bank or timing. Repeat the method across several settled transactions before treating the result as a planning range.

Build a currency worksheet that remains auditable

Use one row per payment event. Suggested columns are order or parcel category, event date, source amount and currency, checkout amount and currency, labelled platform lines, authorization amount, settled amount and currency, explicit payment-side fee, adjusted settled amount, observed rate direction, reference source and timestamp, observed difference, refund link and notes.

Add an evidence status: checkout captured, pending only, settled, adjusted or unresolved. A row should not enter averages until it is settled and reconciled. For comparisons, group transactions by the same currency pair and payment method; otherwise, the average can disguise different conversion paths.

Plan future costs with a range rather than copying one observed rate forever. Use a low, central and high planning assumption, label the date, and refresh it before payment. Keep the product, domestic delivery, platform service line, international shipping and destination costs outside the currency-difference row unless your calculation explicitly defines their inclusion.

Escalate when the currency code is missing, the checkout and authorization cannot be matched, an explicit fee is unexplained, the settled amount changes after posting, or a refund lacks a traceable relationship to the original payment. The goal is a reproducible record, not a forced explanation.

PikoBuy currency conversion FAQ

Does PikoBuy publish a fixed exchange rate?

The public terms checked on August 30, 2026 mention exchange-rate fluctuations and settlement timing but do not publish a permanent rate or fixed FX markup. Verify each live checkout and settled transaction.

Which amount should I use for the observed rate?

Use the final settled home-currency amount, remove separately itemized fees, and divide by the matching checkout-currency amount. Keep the direction explicit.

Is a benchmark rate the amount my card should settle?

No. It is a comparison point. Card-network tools themselves describe displayed conversions as indicative and note that issuer fees or processing timing can affect settlement.

How should I record a partial refund?

Create a separate linked event with its source amount, returned amount, currencies and date. Do not blend it into the original purchase rate.
