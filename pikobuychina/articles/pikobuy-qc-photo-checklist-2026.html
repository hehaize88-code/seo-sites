How to Check Pikobuy QC Photos: A Category-by-Category Checklist

Use a structured Pikobuy QC photo review for shoes, clothing, bags and accessories before approving warehouse items for international shipping.

Understand what QC photos can and cannot prove

Warehouse QC photographs are a visual checkpoint between domestic delivery and international shipment. They can reveal the received color, visible shape, labels, measurements, obvious damage and whether basic parts appear to be present. They are especially useful for comparing the delivered item with the option recorded at order time.

They cannot prove everything. A photograph may not show material composition, long-term durability, internal construction, smell, exact comfort or hidden defects. Lighting and camera distance can also affect color and proportion. Treat the images as evidence for visible questions, not as a complete quality guarantee.

Begin every review by reopening the order record. Confirm product name, selected size, color and variant before judging details. Many avoidable mistakes happen because a buyer studies the photographs carefully but compares them with the wrong option or an unrelated promotional image.

Run a first-pass identity check

Use the first pass to answer simple identity questions. Is the item type correct? Does the color family match? Is the size label visible and consistent with the order? Are the expected pieces included? Does the quantity match? Is there obvious crushing, staining, tearing or breakage?

Compare the complete-item view before zooming into details. Overall proportions often reveal a wrong model or variant faster than close-ups. For pairs or sets, look for symmetry and matching color. For products with removable parts, check that the components shown correspond to the listing and selected option.

If the basic identity is wrong, write a precise note such as “ordered black size 42; warehouse label shows size 41.” A specific discrepancy is easier to act on than a vague comment like “bad QC.” Keep screenshots or image references with the row so the decision remains understandable later.

Shoe QC checklist

For shoes, start with a full view of the pair. Check whether the left and right shoes have similar height, toe shape and overall profile. Compare the side panels, lace placement and major construction lines. Look at the heels together for alignment, shape and consistent branding placement where applicable.

Inspect the toe area for uneven curvature, glue overflow, damaged material or visible creasing. Review stitching paths and panel edges. A small difference caused by camera angle should not be confused with a construction defect, so compare multiple views before deciding.

The sole view helps confirm tread pattern, obvious separation and contamination. The size tag should match the selected size and model information recorded with the order. If the pair is intended to include accessories, alternate laces or packaging, check whether the photographs show them or whether the order description explicitly excludes them.

When an important angle is missing, request a targeted image if the current service allows it. “Close-up of both heel tabs together” is more useful than “more photos.” Confirm the current fee and availability before requesting additional media.

Clothing QC checklist

For shirts, hoodies, jackets, trousers and jerseys, check the full front and back first. Confirm the main color, garment type and visible size label. Compare print, embroidery, patches and pockets with the ordered version. Look for obvious skew, incomplete printing, stains and visible damage.

Measurements matter because labeled sizes can differ between listings. Use the available measurement photographs to compare chest width, body length, shoulder width, sleeve length, waist or inseam with your target garment. Record the measurement method because flat width and circumference are not interchangeable.

Inspect collars, cuffs, hems, zippers, buttons and drawstrings. Check whether paired elements sit at similar heights. On printed clothing, compare the print’s scale and centering with the garment, while allowing for wrinkles and camera perspective. On embroidered products, look for loose threads, missing areas and irregular borders that are actually visible.

Do not make unsupported claims about fabric weight or composition from a photograph alone. If a listing provides material information, record it as a seller claim unless the warehouse provides a separate measurement or label image.

Bags and accessory QC checklist

For bags, inspect the complete silhouette, front, back, sides and base. Compare pattern or panel alignment, corner shape, handle placement and strap attachment. Review zippers, clasps, buckles and other hardware for visible damage, missing pieces and major color inconsistency.

Ask whether the lining, interior pockets or serial-style labels are relevant to your decision. If they are, make sure the existing photographs show them. For small accessories, confirm dimensions because product photos can make scale difficult to judge. Use a ruler image or warehouse measurement when available.

For watches, electronics or products with model-specific parts, a photograph may confirm appearance and labeling but not full operation. Check the current inspection service before assuming functional testing is included. Shipping restrictions can also differ for batteries, liquids, magnets or other controlled items, so visual approval does not automatically mean route eligibility.

Turn observations into a decision

Separate observations from outcomes. First write what is visible: “right heel appears lower in two rear images” or “chest width measures approximately 56 cm in the provided image.” Then choose the action: approve, request another image, ask for an exchange, request a return or hold the item. This keeps the decision auditable.

Consider materiality. A minor loose thread may be acceptable to one buyer and unacceptable to another. Define your priorities before reviewing: correct size, correct color, no major damage and specific design details, for example. The checklist should support your tolerance rather than pretend every visible difference has the same importance.

Respect the current return window and service rules. Do not delay a decision while waiting for information that cannot be obtained. If an extra-photo request is available, make it precise and timely. If it is unavailable, decide based on the evidence you actually have.

Keep a useful QC record

Save the final QC status with the product row, date and reason. A short record helps when several items are being consolidated into one parcel and prevents an unreviewed item from being shipped accidentally. It also creates examples for future orders from the same category or seller.

After delivery, compare the received item with the warehouse photographs. Note whether measurements, color and visible details were represented accurately. This feedback improves later decisions and helps you refine the checklist around issues that matter most to you.

Use the same viewing conditions whenever possible. Review the full-resolution files rather than relying only on small thumbnails, and check more than one angle before treating a shape difference as real. If a photograph is blurred, strongly tilted or affected by glare, record that limitation. A precise note about weak evidence is better than a confident conclusion drawn from an image that cannot support it.

The strongest QC process is consistent, category-specific and evidence-based. It does not promise certainty. It creates a deliberate checkpoint before international shipping, when visible problems may still be easier to address.
