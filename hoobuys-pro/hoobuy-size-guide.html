Hoobuy Size Guide: Read Shoe and Clothing Charts Before Ordering

The most useful Hoobuy size guide starts with the exact seller's measurement chart, not a universal conversion rule. Hoobuy provides access to products from third-party listings, and those listings can use different size labels, measuring methods and garment shapes. An M in one listing does not establish the dimensions of an M in another. The goal is to compare the same physical measurement at every stage.

Use this method before choosing a product from the finds page. Keep a reference measurement, the seller's chart and any later warehouse measurement in separate columns. That makes it possible to identify a real difference without confusing body dimensions, garment dimensions and packaging dimensions.

Identify what the chart measures

Read the column labels and any measurement diagram before recording numbers. A chest column can describe full body circumference, garment circumference or flat garment width. A length column can start at the shoulder, the back neckline or another point. Numbers that look plausible may still be unsuitable for comparison when their definitions differ.

For footwear, distinguish foot length, recommended foot length, removable insole length and external sole length. These measure different things. A printed EU, UK or US size is a label within a particular sizing system; it does not prove that two shoes with that label have identical internal dimensions or shape.

Save the chart with the exact product option and date. If the seller changes the chart later, your original record explains the selection you made. When a page contains several garment types or versions, confirm which chart applies to the selected item rather than borrowing the most convenient one.

Choose a useful reference item

For clothing, select a garment you already wear comfortably for the same purpose. Lay it flat without stretching and measure using the seller's stated endpoints. A lightweight fitted T-shirt is a poor reference for the room required in a winter hoodie. Note whether you want similar fit, extra layering room or a deliberately different silhouette.

Record chest width, body length and relevant shoulder or sleeve measurements. For trousers, waist, inseam, rise and thigh width may matter. An elastic waist needs a stated condition, such as relaxed, rather than an unexplained number. Do not compare a stretched maximum with another garment's relaxed measurement.

For shoes, measure both feet with consistent positioning and units and account for the type of sock normally worn. Use familiar footwear as context for shape and width, but do not assume another model's size conversion will carry across. If a particular fit requirement cannot be assessed from the chart, leave it as an open question.

Convert units without changing the meaning

One inch equals exactly 2.54 centimetres. Convert the numbers before comparing them and retain enough precision to avoid unnecessary rounding. For example, a 22-inch flat width is 55.88 centimetres. This calculation only changes the unit; it does not tell you whether the seller measures the garment in the same way.

Keep a unit beside every value. Confusing millimetres with centimetres, or a flat width with a circumference, creates much larger errors than a small rounding difference. Doubling a flat chest width can approximate garment circumference, but the resulting value is still not the wearer's recommended chest size.

Compare several dimensions together

A size may match your reference chest width but be too short through the body or too long in the sleeve. Compare the dimensions that determine your intended fit as a group. Write down which differences are acceptable before deciding between sizes. This prevents a large size letter from becoming a substitute for the actual measurements.

Do not treat a generic “size up” instruction as a reliable rule. A dropped shoulder can change the sleeve measurement while leaving the overall reach similar. A cropped garment may intentionally be shorter. A raglan sleeve requires a different comparison from a conventional shoulder seam. Interpret the design and the measurement convention together.

Seller-stated tolerances should be recorded as statements attached to that listing. Do not invent a universal acceptable deviation or assume all sellers use the same manual-measurement allowance. If your decision depends on a narrow margin, additional evidence may be more useful than pretending the chart is exact.

Build a measurement ledger

Create one row per relevant dimension with columns for the reference item, seller chart, selected size, warehouse observation, unit and measuring method. Add the date and the source of each value. A blank measurement is unknown, not zero. The ledger makes it clear whether the uncertainty comes from missing data or an actual mismatch.

As an illustrative comparison, a reference hoodie might have a 56 cm relaxed flat chest width while a seller chart lists 57 cm for the chosen size. This does not prove fit: body length, sleeve construction, fabric behaviour and measuring method still matter. The numbers are an example of how to organise evidence, not a recommendation for any specific body size.

Request warehouse measurements precisely

When measurement services are available, identify the item, measurement endpoints and condition. A useful request asks for the garment laid flat with the tape visible from one side seam to the other at the stated chest position. For body length, specify the starting and ending points. Avoid asking only for a “size check.”

For footwear, explain whether you need an insole measurement or another supported measurement and why it matters. Do not ask the warehouse to guarantee comfort from a photograph. Some constructions make removal or accurate measurement impractical; the absence of a usable measurement should remain an explicit uncertainty.

Review the image itself. A folded garment, curved tape, hidden endpoint or perspective distortion can make a displayed number misleading. If the method differs from the seller chart, resolve that difference before deciding the product is incorrectly sized. The QC checklist explains how to request a targeted follow-up.

Resolve an unsuitable size before international shipping

Compare the received size label with the selected option, then compare the useful measurements. A matching label does not settle a large physical mismatch, and a small measurement difference does not automatically establish a defect. Keep the order evidence and warehouse images together when asking support to investigate.

Check the current return or exchange conditions and deadlines for the actual order. Do not assume that an item can be returned after parcel submission or that every size issue qualifies for free return shipping. Preserve the garment's condition and packaging while the decision is open, and avoid paying international freight for an item you already consider unsuitable.

Keep expectations within the available evidence

Measurements cannot fully predict softness, stretch recovery, shrinkage, foot pressure or long-term comfort. Seller descriptions and care labels can add information, but they do not remove every uncertainty. Record the remaining limitations and decide whether they are acceptable before approving the order.

Use the shoe guide and hoodie guide for category-specific checks. Save the final measurement decision in your spreadsheet, then move to the parcel-planning stage. The purpose of a size guide is a traceable, better-informed choice, not an impossible promise of perfect fit.
