<h2>Actual weight and volumetric weight answer different questions</h2>
<p>Actual weight is what the packed parcel weighs. Volumetric weight is a logistics calculation for parcels that occupy substantial space relative to their mass. Hoobuy's current estimator states that most air-cargo volume weight is calculated as length × width × height divided by 6,000, with centimetres producing kilograms. A 40 × 30 × 20 cm parcel therefore has a volumetric result of 4 kg, even if the scale result is lower.</p>
<div class="formula"><strong>Editorial planning formula based on the current public estimator</strong><br>Volume weight (kg) = length (cm) × width (cm) × height (cm) ÷ 6,000<br><br>Always use the divisor and billing method displayed for the selected live route. A route may apply its own chargeable-weight rules.</div>
<p>Bulky but light goods deserve special attention. Shoe boxes, down jackets, plush products and rigid cases can occupy more space than their mass suggests. Removing disposable packaging or compressing suitable clothing may reduce dimensions, but only choose a service after considering protection and product value. A collectible box, structured bag or fragile item may need space rather than compression.</p>

<h2>Category affects route eligibility</h2>
<p>A route is not available merely because the parcel fits its weight limit. Item attributes can matter. Hoobuy's current estimator asks for an item category and notes limitations involving prohibited goods, medicines, food, liquids, creams, granules and pastes. Electronics may involve batteries or magnetic components. Branded or sensitive goods can face different route policies. Do not hide an item category to force a cheaper route; an inaccurate declaration can create delays, returns or seizure risk.</p>
<p>Build the category record while selecting products. Mark batteries, liquids, fragile construction, large rigid packaging and other special attributes before consolidation. When warehouse information becomes available, reconcile the mark with the actual item. If a route disappears after a category is applied, treat that as an eligibility result rather than a technical error.</p>

<h2>Use first-weight and additional-weight billing carefully</h2>
<p>Many lines charge a first weight unit and then increments above it. The first unit is the minimum billable block, not a free allowance. A light parcel can therefore be expensive per kilogram, while combining compatible items may spread the first-unit cost. That does not mean every item should be consolidated. A bulky or restricted product can remove useful routes for the rest of the parcel.</p>
<p>Compare at least two parcel scenarios. The first is a consolidated parcel containing compatible items. The second separates any unusually bulky, fragile or restricted item. Record the live route, chargeable weight and optional service cost for each scenario. Choose the result that fits both cost and risk, not simply the lowest headline figure.</p>

<h2>Packaging decisions should be item-specific</h2>
<ul class="check">
<li><strong>Shoes:</strong> decide whether the original box is essential. Without it, inspect the risk of crushing and protect the pair appropriately.</li>
<li><strong>Clothing:</strong> bulky garments may benefit from compression when the material and construction allow it.</li>
<li><strong>Bags:</strong> a soft bag may fold; a structured bag can deform and may need internal support.</li>
<li><strong>Electronics:</strong> confirm battery or magnetic restrictions, accessories and suitable protective packaging.</li>
<li><strong>Fragile items:</strong> plan cushioning and separation; a smaller parcel is not automatically a safer parcel.</li>
</ul>
<p>Selected value-added services can add cost. Hoobuy's public general-cost page explicitly says displayed shipping prices exclude selected value-added service fees. Record these services beside the parcel estimate so a later difference is not mistaken for an unexplained freight change.</p>

<h2>What this guide does not promise</h2>
<p>No static article can promise a route, delivery time, customs outcome or final charge. International logistics involves carrier operations, customs and changing service conditions. A useful guide narrows uncertainty: it ensures that the parcel record contains the correct destination, categories, measurements, chosen services and dated route evidence. It does not turn that evidence into a guarantee.</p>
<p>The best time to reduce shipping cost is often before parcel submission: choose products with realistic dimensions, avoid unnecessary rigid packaging, separate route-sensitive goods when helpful and compare scenarios using current warehouse data. Once the parcel is submitted, preserve the transaction record and track changes rather than rebuilding the budget from memory.</p>
