Hoobuy Spreadsheet With QC: Links, Photos & Price Checks

A Hoobuy spreadsheet with QC should separate the seller listing from warehouse evidence for the exact item received. Save the current URL, selected option, source price, size evidence and check date before purchase; add warehouse photos and an approval decision after arrival.

Use the dedicated shoe spreadsheet guide, hoodie spreadsheet guide and size-chart guide for the checks that differ by category.

A strong Hoobuy spreadsheet should reduce uncertainty, not disguise it. Many product lists contain an image, a short name and one link. That is enough to create a click, but not enough to support a buying decision. Listings expire, sellers reuse photos, variants carry different prices and a product that looked correct in a table can arrive at the warehouse with a different size label or missing accessory.

The safer approach treats every spreadsheet row as a dated evidence record. It identifies the current destination page, exact option, amount seen, image match, check date and later warehouse evidence. It also distinguishes between facts copied from a live page and editorial notes such as a QC reminder or approximate USD conversion.

Start with the route, not the marketing name

A product name can be vague or duplicated. The route is the complete destination URL that opens the current listing. Save it in full and open it before adding the row. Confirm that the page returns normally, the product shown matches the intended image and the relevant options are still selectable. A route that opens a homepage, search result or unrelated product is not verified.

Use the actual destination title in the name field. You may shorten it for a card, but keep the full source title in a separate column. This makes it easier to detect when an image is reused across different sellers or when a short label such as “latest shoe find” hides several models.

Do not use zero when an amount or weight is unknown. Zero is a value; unknown is an evidence gap. This simple distinction prevents false totals and makes future rechecks much faster.

Price needs a timestamp and currency

The headline figure on a listing may belong to the cheapest option. Choose the intended model, size, colour and quantity before recording a price. Keep the source currency. If you also show USD, label it approximate and save the conversion rate or date used. A conversion is an editorial calculation, not the amount the checkout promises.

Separate product price, seller-to-warehouse freight, any identified order adjustment and later international parcel cost. Combining them in one “price” column prevents fair comparison. A cheaper item can have higher domestic freight, and a bulky item can raise a later parcel's chargeable weight. The domestic cost guide explains how to keep those stages distinct.

Images must match the same live product

Use the first image from the verified destination listing, not an image copied from an old spreadsheet. Then check that the image URL loads independently and belongs to the same page. Give it a descriptive alt attribute that identifies the product rather than writing “image” or an internal ID.

An image match does not prove that the physical item will be identical. It only confirms that the destination card and listing are aligned at the time of checking. Warehouse QC remains the physical evidence stage. Record the source-image check and warehouse-image check separately.

Write one concrete QC prompt per category

A generic note such as “check quality” is not actionable. Shoes need model, size label, outsole, pair symmetry and visible glue or stitching checks. Clothing needs measurements, size label, seams, print or embroidery placement and fabric-related evidence. Bags need dimensions, corners, closures, strap hardware and included pieces. Electronics require model and accessory checks plus route-sensitive attributes such as batteries; a photograph alone may not prove function.

The spreadsheet prompt should stay short because the full method belongs in the QC checklist. Its job is to make the buyer inspect the right evidence before approving international shipping.

Do not label ordinary listing photos as QC

Seller images describe an offer. Warehouse images show the item received after purchase. Calling both “QC photos” makes the spreadsheet misleading. Use separate columns for source image, warehouse image availability and QC decision. A clean workflow moves through states such as “not ordered,” “ordered,” “warehouse received,” “QC review needed,” “approved,” “return requested” and “parcel submitted.”

Hoobuy's current public forwarding guide says forwarded packages are categorized, photographed for QC and stored at the warehouse, and that accurate domestic tracking numbers help match product information. This supports recording tracking and warehouse IDs rather than relying only on the original link.

Manage stale links instead of hiding them

A spreadsheet grows weaker when old rows remain “verified” forever. Use a recheck schedule based on importance. High-traffic or homepage products should be checked frequently. Lower-priority items can be checked before publication and again when a user intends to order.

  • 200 and same product: update the checked date after reviewing options and image.
  • Redirect to same product: save the final URL and confirm the page content.
  • Homepage or unrelated result: mark the row unavailable and rematch manually.
  • 404: remove the purchase button until an exact replacement is found.
  • Changed title or image: treat it as a new evidence review, not a minor edit.

Never replace only the domain while keeping an unverified product ID. Identical-looking URL structures across sites do not guarantee identical IDs. An exact remap requires the name, image and destination page to agree.

Add warehouse and parcel fields only when evidence exists

After arrival, add the warehouse item ID, measured weight, dimensions, QC review result and any requested details. Do not overwrite the listing data; the difference between what was offered and what arrived can be useful. If the item proceeds to shipping, record its parcel ID, selected route and final decision in a linked parcel sheet.

Hoobuy's current estimation page uses destination, weight, item category and dimensions to check available routes. That means a pre-order spreadsheet should flag category and likely bulk, but it should not present a final international price. The shipping guide shows how to turn warehouse measurements into a useful parcel comparison.

A practical row review before publishing

  1. Open the destination page and confirm it returns normally.
  2. Match the full title and current first image.
  3. Select the intended option before recording an amount.
  4. Store source currency and label any USD conversion approximate.
  5. Assign the correct category and one specific QC prompt.
  6. Add a check date and reviewer note.
  7. Test the product button and image URL.
  8. Do not add a product if the exact destination cannot be verified.

What a trustworthy spreadsheet looks like

A trustworthy table may contain fewer products than a scraped list, but each row is useful. The destination opens the intended product, the image matches, the title is accurate, the price evidence is transparent and the QC prompt is specific. When a link expires, the row says so. When warehouse evidence arrives, the record separates it from seller marketing.

This approach also creates better search content. Category pages can explain real inspection differences, product cards contain meaningful names and alt text, and guides can answer a buyer's next question instead of repeating the same generic FAQ. The spreadsheet becomes an evidence system rather than a collection of clicks.
