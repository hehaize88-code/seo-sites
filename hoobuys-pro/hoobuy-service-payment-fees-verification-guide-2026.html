The safest answer to “What are HooBuy's fees?” is not one percentage copied from a screenshot. A reverse-shopping-agent transaction can contain several cost layers created at different times: the seller's product amount, China domestic freight, a payment or balance movement, selected warehouse services, international shipping and any destination-side cost. Some may be zero for a particular order; others may be unknown until the relevant stage.

A cost planner should verify each layer from the live interface where it becomes actionable. This article shows how to do that without claiming a fixed service fee, payment surcharge, exchange-rate markup or coupon benefit that the current public sources do not establish. The outcome is a ledger that explains what was quoted, what you confirmed and what finally settled.

Start with a fee taxonomy, not a total

A checkout total can be mathematically correct and still be hard to audit if every adjustment is placed in one “fees” cell. Separate charges by cause and stage. A seller-side amount is not an agent service. A payment-method difference is not domestic freight. A packaging option is not included merely because it appears near a shipping quote.

Record four states for every amount

Use estimated, quoted, confirmed and settled as separate states. An estimate is a planning number based on incomplete inputs. A quote is the amount shown for a specific configuration at a specific time. Confirmed means you accepted that transaction. Settled means the balance or payment provider shows the final movement.

Never overwrite the estimate with the settled amount. Keep both and record the difference. This makes the budget error visible and prevents you from misremembering an early calculator output as a promise. The seller-to-warehouse cost guide applies the same state model to China domestic freight.

Zero versus unknown: enter zero only when the relevant live record explicitly shows no charge. Leave the field unknown when no amount is displayed. Treating unknown as zero is the fastest way to understate a landed-cost plan.

Verify the product-order screen

Before payment, compare the exact source, selected option, quantity, item amount and domestic freight. Note any seller adjustment or changed price in its own row. A headline listing amount may apply to another variant or quantity. The agent order is the better evidence for what you are about to authorize, but preserve the source record to explain any difference.

Look for separately named service or handling lines. Do not infer that an omitted line is permanently free, and do not relabel a seller cost as an agent fee. Record the currency displayed and the time checked because the order can change before confirmation.

Verify payment and top-up at the final method screen

HooBuy's public help center distinguishes Payment, Top up and International Credit Card topics. That taxonomy confirms these are decision areas, but it does not prove that every method has the same availability, fee treatment, settlement path or currency result. Select the actual method you intend to use and review the final payable amount before authorizing it.

Capture three values where available: the amount credited to the account or order, the amount charged by the platform flow, and the amount ultimately posted by your payment provider. If currencies differ, preserve both currency codes and amounts. Do not calculate a supposed platform exchange markup from one screenshot unless the comparison uses the same timestamp, base amount and clearly identified rates.

Your bank or payment provider may apply its own conversion, card or cross-border treatment. That is not automatically a HooBuy fee. Keep provider-side adjustments in a separate field and use the provider statement as evidence. Avoid storing full card numbers, security codes or unnecessary account details in the ledger.

Audit a top-up and an order payment differently

A top-up moves value into an account balance; an order payment allocates value to a purchase. If both appear in the history, do not count both as product cost. Link the top-up to the later balance deduction. Record any returned balance or refund as a movement, not as a negative product price until you know which order it belongs to.

For reconciliation, use a simple equation: opening balance plus settled credits minus settled debits equals closing balance. If it does not match, look for pending, reversed, refunded or separately denominated movements. Do not force the equation by creating a generic “fee” line with no supporting transaction.

Confirm selected warehouse services one by one

HooBuy's general shipping-cost page states that displayed shipping prices do not include value-added service fees and that selected services are calculated separately. This supports a practical rule: a service is part of your cost only when you select it and the order shows its amount. Do not add a universal packaging, reinforcement, photo or handling charge from another buyer's record.

For each service, record the service name exactly, affected item or parcel, quoted amount, confirmation date and final deduction. If a service changes package dimensions, it may also change the later international quote. Keep the direct service charge separate from its effect on billable weight.

Do not audit international shipping too early

The public HooBuy shipping information says category and oversized dimensions can affect price, and selected value-added services are extra. Final route comparison therefore needs the destination, packed weight, dimensions, item categories and service selections. A pre-warehouse estimate is useful for scenario planning but cannot prove the final parcel charge.

Use the actual weight, volume and route guide after warehouse measurements exist. Preserve the estimator date and assumptions, then compare them with the submitted parcel. If the final amount changes, identify which input changed before assigning the difference to an unexplained fee.

Run a pre-payment fee check

  1. Confirm the exact item, variant, quantity and source currency.
  2. Separate the seller's domestic freight from the product amount.
  3. List only service lines visible for this order or parcel.
  4. Select the intended payment method and record the final payable amount before authorization.
  5. If currencies differ, record both amounts and who performed the conversion where the evidence shows it.
  6. Save the quote date and status; do not call a pending authorization settled.
  7. After payment, reconcile the account ledger and payment-provider statement.
  8. Keep international shipping and destination-side costs outside the product-payment subtotal.

Use a worked ledger without invented rates

Suppose an order shows a product amount, a separate domestic freight line and a final payment amount. Enter those displayed values exactly. If the payment amount differs from the sum, create an “unclassified difference” row until the interface or statement identifies it. Do not assume the difference is a service fee, conversion fee or tax.

Later, when a warehouse service is selected, add it as a new confirmed row. When the parcel is submitted, add the selected route and service amounts using the packed record. The landed total is the sum of settled rows, not every estimate you produced along the way. An estimate can remain beside it for accuracy review.

Red flags in fee claims

  • A single percentage is presented without payment method, currency, date or transaction stage.
  • A shipping estimate is described as the final charge before packing.
  • A provider-side card adjustment is automatically labeled a platform fee.
  • A top-up and the later balance payment are both counted as separate spending.
  • A coupon or promotion is treated as available without current eligibility evidence.
  • An unknown amount is entered as zero to make the budget look complete.
  • A value-added service is assumed included because it appears on the parcel page.

The reliable answer is a traceable total

A fee guide is useful when it tells you where to look and how to reconcile what you find. The publicly accessible sources confirm that HooBuy separates fees, payment, top-up, cards, delivery and warehouse services, and that selected value-added services can sit outside displayed shipping prices. They do not support publishing one universal numerical fee schedule for every case.

Build the answer from your own live transaction: classify the layer, preserve the quote, confirm the chosen action and reconcile the settled record. That method will not predict every future amount, but it will stop estimates, payment movements and shipping services from being counted twice or mislabeled.
