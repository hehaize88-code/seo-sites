Hipobuy Size Guide UK: Shoes, Clothing and QC Measurements

Choose Hipobuy shoe and clothing sizes using the exact seller chart, reference measurements and warehouse QC before shipping to the UK.

6 October 2026 · HipoBuys UK Editorial Desk · 6 min read

A UK size label is a starting point, not enough evidence to choose a Hipobuy item. The source seller may use a different size system, the listing may show several charts, and the available warehouse photograph may reveal only the label. A useful Hipobuy size guide therefore connects three things: a measurement you trust, the chart for the exact listing, and the item that arrives at the warehouse. This guide explains that process for UK shoe and clothing buyers without pretending that one conversion table fits every seller.

Begin with the item you intend to wear, including its purpose. A fitted shirt, a roomy hoodie and a winter layer require different allowances. For footwear, the shape of the toe box and width matter alongside length. Record the result you want before comparing options, so an attractive product photograph does not become the only reason for accepting a questionable size.

Separate UK, EU, US and centimetre labels

Read the heading above every size column. A number can identify a UK shoe size, a US men's size, a US women's size, a European size or a measurement in centimetres. Those are not interchangeable labels. Even a centimetre figure needs a definition: it might refer to foot length, an insole or a size code printed on the product. For example, Nike's own footwear guidance distinguishes the centimetre marking on a label from measured foot length. That distinction is a reason to read the relevant chart, not a universal conversion rule for other products.

Save the listing's chart with the selected variant and the date. If the product page includes several images with different measurements, identify which chart belongs to that model. Ask for clarification before payment when the evidence conflicts. Do not average incompatible charts or assume the larger number is safer. Buying a longer shoe does not necessarily solve insufficient width, and a larger jacket may change sleeve and shoulder fit as well as chest room.

Create a reference measurement at home

For clothing, use a garment you already find comfortable and compare like with like. Lay it flat without stretching it. Note the point from which each measurement starts and where it ends. A pit-to-pit width is not the same measurement as a body chest circumference; doubling a flat width can help comparison only when both charts use compatible methods. Keep the original flat measurement in your notes so the interpretation remains visible.

For footwear, follow the measuring instructions attached to the brand or seller chart you plan to use. Measure both feet and record any meaningful difference. Keep foot measurements separate from measurements of a removable insole. A curved heel cup, a padded toe or a shaped insole can make a photograph difficult to interpret. When you cannot establish an equivalent measurement, mark the comparison uncertain instead of converting it into a precise recommendation.

Write a compact size record before ordering

The most useful record is small enough to check again when QC arrives. Include the listing, exact variant, chart size, measuring method and your acceptable range. For a hoodie, you might record chest width, body length and sleeve length. For trousers, add waistband measurement, rise and inseam. For shoes, record the size system and the chart's stated relationship to foot length. Avoid adding fields that will never affect your decision.

An illustrative clothing comparison shows why this helps. Suppose a comfortable hoodie measures 58 cm across the chest when laid flat, while a listing gives 56 cm for one option and 60 cm for another. Neither is automatically correct: the first might be too fitted for layering, while the second could be wider than intended. Compare length and sleeves before choosing. These figures demonstrate a method; they are not a size recommendation for any product in the directory.

Use warehouse QC to answer a specific fit question

A clear QC request identifies the item, measurement and endpoints. For a top, request a straight measurement across the chest with the garment laid flat. For trousers, specify whether you need an unstretched waistband, an inseam or a full outside length. For a shoe, say whether the uncertainty concerns the label, a removable insole or visible width. A request for “better pictures” leaves too much room for an unhelpful result.

Check available services and their current price before requesting extra work. Do not assume that every measurement, additional photograph or video is included. When the photograph arrives, look for the start of the ruler, a readable scale and a consistent garment position. A ruler held diagonally or partly outside the frame may not answer the original question. Ask for a correction that identifies the exact issue rather than collecting several equally ambiguous photographs.

Match the photograph to the ordered variant

Start the QC review with identity. Confirm the item, colour, quantity and selected size against the order record. A readable size label is useful evidence of what the warehouse received, but it does not prove fit or establish that the seller followed a particular chart. Compare the label and measured result together. If the label is correct but the dimensions are materially different, preserve both pieces of evidence.

Keep pairs and multiple items separate. Two black hoodies or similar shoe boxes can be confused when photographs are saved without context. Use a private filename that connects each image to the relevant order, without putting personal details into a public album. The UK QC checklist covers visible condition checks; the purpose here is to make the size decision before international shipping limits the available options.

Make an accept, clarify or return decision

Accept only when the available evidence supports the fit you intended. Clarify when the measurement method, chart identity or photograph is incomplete. Consider a return request when the confirmed item does not meet your requirements, subject to the seller's eligibility rules and the current order status. These are three different decisions, and a pending clarification should not silently become shipping approval.

Hipobuy's public returns guidance ties eligibility to conditions including seller participation, item condition and the warehouse return window. That makes early inspection important. Do not wait until the end of a storage period to investigate size, and do not request removal of tags or packaging while a return remains possible. The UK returns and refunds guide explains how to keep the decision and the refund process separate.

Treat material and silhouette as additional evidence

Measurements explain dimensions, not every aspect of comfort. Fabric stretch, lining, padding, shoulder construction and intended silhouette can change how an item feels. Record what the seller actually states and distinguish it from what a photograph appears to show. A close-up may help identify a visible construction detail; it cannot reliably establish long-term durability or the precise composition of a fabric.

Use customer sizing comments cautiously. A review that says “true to size” is difficult to apply without the person's usual size system, measurements, selected variant and preferred fit. A report about another seller or production version is even less direct. Treat such comments as questions to investigate, rather than replacing the chart and your own reference garment with a popular recommendation.

Keep the size decision connected to the parcel

Before submitting a UK-bound parcel, confirm that every included item has a completed size decision. Check that an item awaiting clarification has not been added accidentally. Keep boxes and protective packaging until the return question is resolved; any later packaging change should be considered separately against protection and shipping cost. The cheapest parcel is poor value if it contains an avoidable fit mistake.

For a future purchase, save the final label, useful measurements and your experience after delivery. Record the exact model and seller so the note does not become a universal rule. A small personal reference built from matching evidence is more useful than repeatedly guessing from UK-to-EU conversion charts. Start with the product categories, then use a size record for the specific listing you choose.
