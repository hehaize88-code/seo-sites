Hipobuy Delivered but Not Received in the UK: A Practical Evidence Plan

Resolve a Hipobuy parcel marked delivered but missing in the UK: verify the carrier, delivery evidence, address and investigation process.

6 October 2026 · HipoBuys UK Editorial Desk · 6 min read

The tracking page says delivered, but the parcel is not where you expected it. Start by identifying which shipment the message describes. A Hipobuy order can have a seller-to-warehouse number, an international parcel number and a local delivery reference. A delivered event on the Chinese domestic shipment does not mean the international parcel has reached your UK address. That first distinction can resolve apparent contradictions before you contact anyone.

If the event belongs to the UK last-mile carrier, keep the exact wording, date, time and reference. Check the destination information in your private parcel record and preserve any available delivery photograph or recipient name. Do not mark receipt in your shopping account merely to clear an alert while the parcel is missing. The aim is to establish the delivery event and request an investigation with a coherent record.

Confirm the carrier and the number

Open the parcel record and identify the local operator actually assigned to that shipment. Do not assume every UK line uses Royal Mail, Evri or the carrier another buyer mentioned. A route label, a handoff message and a working local tracking number provide more useful evidence than a generic estimate page. Keep the original international reference as well, because it connects the earlier journey.

If the local number is unclear, ask the shipping service to identify it. Explain that the international record shows delivery but you need to verify the UK handoff and final event. Avoid entering unrelated order numbers into multiple tracking websites and treating whichever result appears as a match. The record should connect the carrier, destination and parcel you actually submitted.

Check the delivery evidence against your address

Compare the address and postcode you entered with the parcel record. Look for a missing flat number, an old address or a workplace destination. A public tracking page may show limited address information, so use your private submission record for the complete comparison. If you find an error, report it accurately rather than changing your account address and assuming the dispatched label will update.

Review any delivery photograph, signature name, safe-place note or collection-point instruction. A photo can show a location that needs clarification without proving deliberate wrongdoing. Describe the mismatch specifically: an unfamiliar doorway, a different building number or a location you cannot identify. Do not publish a neighbour's face, private address or signature while asking for help online.

Make a short local check

Check with members of the household and any authorised recipient. For a shared building, ask reception or the parcel room. For a workplace, check the mailroom or colleagues who handle deliveries. Inspect the safe place you actually nominated and any location identified by the delivery evidence. Keep a note of what you checked and when so the same steps do not need to be repeated later.

Royal Mail's own guidance for delivered-but-unreceived items recommends address, recipient and safe-place checks, and notes that some delivery photographs are available through tracking. It also explains that a same-day scan can occasionally be erroneous and advises allowing an additional day in that specific situation. Follow the actual carrier's current guidance, while separately preserving any platform reporting deadline; do not turn one carrier's advice into a universal waiting rule.

Notify the relevant services without losing time

Once the basic checks are complete, use the verified carrier contact route and the Hipobuy parcel support process as appropriate. State that the delivery event is disputed and ask for the applicable investigation step. The carrier may need the sender or contracting shipping service to raise part of the case. If so, pass that requirement to parcel support and retain the reference.

Do not assume that opening one enquiry automatically registers every possible claim. A delivery investigation, a platform after-sales case and an insurance application can be separate processes. Ask what has actually been opened and what information is still required. Keep the reporting date and the next action visible, especially if a reply asks you to wait for another party.

Write a factual missing-delivery report

A strong report contains the parcel reference, local tracking number, selected service, disputed scan and delivery address confirmation through the private channel. Add the evidence reviewed, the checks already made and the reason the parcel remains unlocated. Request a specific action such as checking the delivery location or confirming the next investigation stage. Avoid unrelated order history that obscures the issue.

For example, you can explain that the delivery photograph does not match your entrance, nobody at the address accepted the parcel and reception has checked its log. That supplies observable facts. It is more useful than assuming the driver stole the parcel or declaring that the whole tracking system is false. If you later find the package, update the same case so the record remains accurate.

Keep transport loss separate from post-delivery disappearance

The distinction matters because the public Hipobuy compensation guidance lists exclusions relating to parcels stolen or missing after delivery. A disputed delivery location, an incorrect scan and a parcel removed after correct delivery are not automatically the same event. Preserve the evidence without prematurely classifying the cause or promising yourself that protection will pay.

Use the parcel insurance and claims guide to organise applicable terms and evidence. If the service concludes that the parcel was delivered correctly, ask for the basis of that conclusion and the available review process. If it establishes a misdelivery or loss, retain the written result. The correct next step should follow the evidence and terms, rather than a generic promise attached to the word insurance.

Handle shared addresses and collection points carefully

At a shared address, establish who is authorised to accept parcels and whether a handover log exists. A recipient name you do not recognise may belong to reception, but it should be checked rather than assumed. For a collection point, distinguish arrival at the location from collection by a recipient. Ask the carrier which event the status represents and what identification process applies.

Do not try to obtain someone else's private delivery records or confront a neighbour based only on an unclear photograph. Make a reasonable enquiry and let the carrier investigate the specific delivery event. Keep any communication brief and factual. If a report to another authority is appropriate under the circumstances, preserve the supporting record and use the relevant official process rather than embellishing the account.

Protect yourself from fee and redelivery messages

A missing parcel makes an unexpected payment message feel plausible. Check any fee or redelivery request through the known carrier channel before acting. A message containing your tracking number is not by itself proof that the payment destination is legitimate. Royal Mail's security guidance warns about impersonated delivery notifications and fee requests, which is why the verified tracking record should remain your starting point.

Never send passwords, payment security codes or one-time authentication codes to someone claiming they can release the parcel. If a genuine charge exists, establish what it relates to and use the service's own process. Keep that charge separate from a missing-delivery investigation; paying an unrelated message will not correct a disputed scan or establish where the goods were left.

Maintain one timeline until the parcel is located or the case closes

Record each contact date, case reference, response and requested next step. Save changed tracking events, not just the initial delivered screen. If an investigation result arrives, connect it to the original parcel and check whether any further action is required within a deadline. If the parcel turns up damaged, retain the packaging and document the condition before switching to a damage report.

For future shipments, review the UK address checklist, choose delivery preferences through the actual carrier and keep household arrangements clear. Those steps cannot guarantee delivery, but they reduce avoidable ambiguity. A complete evidence trail gives support a defined problem to investigate and keeps you from confusing a warehouse delivery, a local handoff and a final receipt.

Keep the exact item at the centre of your plan.
